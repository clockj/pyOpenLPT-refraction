- **The detector works on these images:**

  - **The effect:** the small ball's centre moves by 0.6 px RMS (up to 3 px) when the rod mask changes from 50 to 40 px, and by 2 px RMS at 60 px (T1 b).
- **The calibration (delivered in `Results`; redelivered on 6 Oct, night, see above):**
  - reprojection RMS **0.47 px** (first delivery 0.45 px);
  - wand length of a free triangulation: **23 µm RMS** (0.23 %; first delivery 22 µm);
  - cam1: f 38,551 px, 1,007 mm from the tube's axis (38,678 px, 1,010 mm);
  - cam2: f 35,812 px, 1,036 mm from the axis, 60.8° around the tube from cam1 (36,022 px, 1,042 mm);
  - the results page shows no warnings.
- **This recording does not determine f (T3 fails).**
  - **The spread:** the halves and quarters of the recording give cam1 f from 32,800 to 40,200 px and cam2 from 30,000 to 40,100 px (41,300 before the redelivery). The calibration's error bars of 0.8 % and 1.2 % are 10–30 times too small.
  - **f and distance move together:** each time the camera's distance from the tube changes in proportion, so f / distance stays nearly constant.
  - **Why:** this is the near-telecentric valley. Long lenses (about 800 mm effective) look at a 30 mm volume from 1 m away. The only information that separates f from distance is the change of magnification across the volume (about ±1.5 %) and the tube's refraction. Errors of a few tenths of a pixel that change with the wand's pose swamp both.
- **Inside the volume the wand covered, the cameras agree much better (D8).**
  - **Wand lengths:** each half's cameras reproduce the other half's with a mean error of 0.07–0.09 % and 26–36 µm RMS
    (0.07–0.13 % and 25–38 µm before the rerun of 7 Oct).
  - **Reprojection:** 0.19–0.29 px from a two-camera triangulation.
  - **What this means:** the delivered camFiles can be used for measurements inside that volume, at about the 0.1–0.2 % scale level. Do not rely on f, the cameras' distances, or anything outside the volume.
- **cam1 against the 30 Sep dot-board calibration (T7):**
  - f is 7.0 % lower: 38,551 against 41,459 px (6.7 % in the first delivery);
  - the line of sight's elevation differs by 2.8°.
  - Given T3 this says nothing about whether the camera changed. The dot board's f is the reliable one if the lens and focus are unchanged.
- **Ruled out as causes:**
  - **synchronisation** (D1): the offset is −0.0004 ± 0.0003 frames;
  - **motion blur:** 100 µs exposures at 100 frames/s, so about 0.2 px of smear;
  - **the wand bouncing on its string:** real motion, seen alike by both cameras (D5);
  - **a stretched calibration:** the wand's 3-D direction explains 0.1 % of the length error (D6);
  - **a smooth pattern over the image** (T2).
- **What remains:**
  - **Centre errors** of a few tenths of a pixel that change with the wand's pose within about 5 frames (D7). The rod (T1 b) is the likely main source; the string and its fibres crossing the outlines add to it.
  - **Per-camera image shifts:** each camera's image shifts horizontally by 0.2–0.3 px from frame to frame on its own (D5: not seen by the other camera, perhaps vibration). The fit absorbs them into the wand's positions.

## 4. How the rod settings were set

- **Rod opening (95 px)**, from the opening scan of P0 (`tests/p0_survey_out.txt`).
  - **Method:** five frames per camera, thresholded as the detector does, then opened with discs of 21–141 px.
  - **Lower limit:** the balls come apart from 91 px in cam1 and 81 px in cam2. Below that the rod survives (the rod plus the string's knot).
  - **Upper limit:** the small ball disappears from 121 px in cam1 and 111 px in cam2, since its height is 115 and 103 px.
  - **Choice:** 95 px lies inside both windows. T1 b confirmed it: no camera-frame lost at 95 or 101 px, and 20 of 200 lost at 91 px, the edge of cam1's window.
  - **Effect on the centres:** small. The opening only finds the balls; the centre comes from the edge fit. Moving from 95 to 101 px moved ball A by 0.06 px RMS.
- **Rod mask (50 px)**, first estimated by eye.
  - **The estimate:** the rod looked about half the small ball's height, so a half-width of about 30 px, plus blur and a margin.
  - **What T1 d measured:** the rod is 84–105 px wide in cam1 at the detector's threshold. That is about 2.2 mm, consistent with your 2 mm. The edge blur σ is 5–6 px.
  - **What T13's rule then asks for:** half-width + 2σ ≈ 56 px (cam1) and 50 px (cam2) for a rod near horizontal, and up to about 73 px for steep rods. The tube widens the rod's image across its axis.
  - **Why no value is right:** with a rod this thick, a mask that covers the rod also removes most of the small ball's outline on the rod's side. Its free ellipse is then poorly fixed along the rod, and the centre moves with the mask by 0.6–2 px RMS (T1 b). This is the detector's limit with this wand, not a setting that can be tuned away.

## 6. For the next recording

1. **A wand with a thinner rod**, if there is one: about 1 mm or less for 3–4 mm balls (rod ≤ 1/3 of the small ball). The detector was built and tested for rods about 1/4 of the ball (T13). Larger balls on the same rod would also help.
2. **An independent f.** With two long-lens cameras the wand alone cannot separate f from the distance.
   - **Measure f first:** calibrate each camera's f with the dot board through the tube, as on 30 Sep for cam1. Then hold f in the wand calibration, so that the wand only finds the poses.
   - **Not yet possible:** `cylinder_wand` cannot hold f yet. It is a small library option (`Layout` frees f always) and needs your approval.
   - **A third camera** would also help.
3. **Cover as much depth as the cameras keep sharp, and the whole image.** The change of magnification across the depth is what separates f from distance.
4. **Keep the whole wand in both views and avoid end-on views.** Only 14 % of these frames were usable in both cameras.
5. **Check the camera mounts.** Each camera's image shifts by 0.2–0.3 px horizontally from frame to frame on its own. This is harmless for the calibration, but it adds the same jitter to tracked positions.
6. **Rerun this case's scripts on the new folder** (change `raw_dir` and `out_dir` in `config.json`). Detection takes about 20 min over the network, or a few minutes from a local disk.
