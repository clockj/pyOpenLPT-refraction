## 3. Design: `projection_fit.py`

**Stages 0–3 are unchanged.** The data, the affine start, the tube placement and the coarse fit use back-projection
only.

**Stage 4's residual** (`evaluate_by_projection`, a copy of `cylinder_wand.evaluate` with one line changed):
1. For each observation, the C++ projection of its wand end in its camera returns every image. The camera is rebuilt
   with `to_cpp` at each evaluation, which takes 0.1 ms.
2. The image nearest the detection is taken. In the single-image region it is the only one.
3. That image is the start of `cyl_model.project`. It polishes the image (planning probe: the two agree to
   7×10⁻⁹ px) and returns the exact derivatives by implicit differentiation.
4. If the C++ projection finds no image, Newton starts at the detection instead, as in the library (added
   2026-10-04, section 7). The C++ does not look outside the water, while the library continues the ray past the
   inner wall. Without this fallback the variant would refuse steps that the library accepts. A wand end that the
   library cannot project either, one in a no-image band, is a failed residual, as now.

What changes against the library is only where Newton starts: at the paper's image, not at the detection. The
residual and its derivatives are otherwise the library's. Where the C++ finds no image, nothing changes (step 4).

**The single-image rule** (`single_image_rule`) replaces the guard, with the same signature and return value:
- It runs where the library runs its guard: once the rounds of adjustment, reweighting and rejection have
  settled, followed by one more adjustment (the paper's Fig. `fig:wandpipeline`, step 6). Every active
  observation is checked, whatever its radius. This also closes the reminder of section 6
  inside this variant.
- **It runs once** (added 2026-10-04, section 7). It is not repeated between rounds. A census inside the iterations
  would drop whatever the current parameters put outside the region. The next round would no longer feel those
  observations, and nothing would pull the fit back.
- An observation is kept only if its wand end has exactly one image in that camera. Every image the projection
  returns counts, on the sensor or not: that is the physical single-image region.
- The census is taken at the point and at the six points 20 µm away along the axes, as the guard's probes do.
- Frames left with an end seen by fewer than two cameras are dropped, as the guard does (`_drop_thin_frames`).
- **A recount, for the report only** (added 2026-10-04). After the last adjustment the census is taken again, without
  dropping anything. The answer's `info` records the number dropped and the recount. It warns when more than 1 % of
  the active observations were dropped, or when the recount finds an observation outside the region: either the wand
  strayed, or the answer is wrong.
- **Alternative:** count only the images on the sensor. A second image off the sensor cannot be confused with the
  detection. This is your call; the default is the physical region.

**`calibrate_by_projection(data, tube, **kw)`** installs both replacements and calls `cylinder_wand.calibrate`. The
answer carries a flag `info["residuals"] = "projection"`.

**Planning probe** (reference rig, 1000 wand ends, 3 cameras; read-only, in my session scratchpad):

| | per wand end |
|---|---|
| C++ `projectAll` | 0.16 ms |
| `cyl_model.project` with derivatives | 0.0045 ms |

- Every end had exactly one image (r ≤ 31 mm; r_c = 75.62 mm).
- A fit with 3000 observations therefore spends about 0.5 s per evaluation in the projection. That makes a
  calibration about 1–1.5 min instead of 20–40 s.

## 6. Reminder, not fixed (D6)

**`cylinder_wand.multi_image_guard` can skip points with two images.**
- **What it checks.** Only points beyond `Tube.fold_radius()`, which is r_c at C_z = 0, 75.62 mm in this tube.
- **Why that misses points.** Air sets r_c here, and r_c then shrinks for tilted rays:

  | C_z | r_c (mm) |
  |---|---|
  | 0 | 75.62 |
  | 0.1 | 75.46 |
  | 0.3 | 74.04 |
  | 0.5 | 70.65 |

- **A counterexample.** A point at r = 74.5 mm, Δφ = 130.50°, Δz = 357.5 mm, seen from a pinhole 1.1 m from the axis,
  has two far-branch images (C_z = 0.300 and 0.298). `D:\Refraction\code\project_cylinder.py`'s `project()` returns
  both.
- **A second reason.** A multilayer cylinder can fold even inside r_c (paper, Section 6.1).
- **Effect so far.** None on any test so far: in the wand rigs C_z ≈ 0.02, which narrows the band to 7 µm.
- **Fix when you allow it.** Take the census of every observation, as this variant does: a few seconds per round.
- **Logged** in `tests/cylinder_wand/README.md` on 2026-10-04: progress log and open item 8.

## 7. Amendments of 2026-10-04: wand ends outside the region during the fit

**The question.** "will the new method potentially drive the optimize to a wrong parameter region? for example, is it
possible that in fact all wands are not in those region, but during the optimization, some parameter induce those
region?"

**Findings.**
1. **The single-image rule cannot steer the fit while it runs once.** It acts on a converged answer, followed by one
   adjustment, so wrong intermediate parameters never drop anything. Repeated every round, it could: each round would
   drop what the current parameters put outside the region, and the next round would no longer feel those
   observations.
2. **Two images mid-fit are harmless.**
   - Near a small residual, the image nearest the detection is equivalent to the distance from the wand end to the
     detection's back-projected ray, and a pixel has only one ray.
   - In the probe below, an end walked into a two-image band keeps its image, which moves smoothly. The new image
     appears about 1,400 px away.
3. **No image mid-fit stops the step.** The residual fails, and the adjustment refuses any step that makes an active
   residual fail: the flat-wall fit's pinning mechanism.

   | where the estimate lands | library: `cyl_model.project`, Newton from the detection | C++ `projectAll` (this plan before step 4's fallback) |
   |---|---|---|
   | two-image band | follows its image | the same: the image nearest the detection |
   | no-image band | fails | fails |
   | beyond the inner wall (97.63 mm) | continues the ray: images out to 101.5 mm, the farthest tried | no image: the C++ searches only points inside the water |

   The last row is why step 4 now falls back to the library's Newton.
4. **A fit rarely meets these barriers.**
   - **The edge of a no-image band images at the tube's outline.** In the probe that is u ≈ 5,135 px, against a
     2,600 px sensor. An end detected on the sensor would need a residual of thousands of pixels to get there.
   - **The wall** is about 65 mm beyond the reference rig's wand ends, which stay within 31 mm of the axis. The other
     cameras would see a move that large.
   - **The start** (stages 1–3) uses back-projection only.
   - **The exceptions:** ends close to r_c, seen by wide-field cameras that see the tube's outline. No test of the
     campaign has them: T5's clouds stay within about 57 mm of the axis (centred within 12 mm of it, half-size at most
     25 mm). P0(d) therefore builds such a rig.
5. **At a wrong answer the rule drops more, and that lowers the answer's cost.** `calibrate` compares its starts by
   the number of observations kept first, so the drops count against a wrong answer. With one surviving start
   nothing is compared, hence the recount's warning.

**The probe** (2026-10-04, read-only, in my session scratchpad; P0(d) repeats it properly). It used camera 1 of the
reference rig, with points 85 mm from the axis in its height plane (C_z ≈ 0). The C++ census, by angle from the
