## 1. What is known

**From the user**
- Port: acrylic tube, outer diameter 7.9375 in, wall 0.125 in, so R1 = 100.806 mm and R2 = 97.631 mm.
- Media: air, acrylic, water, with the camera in air and the target in water.
- Camera: Phantom VEO710, 1280 x 800 px of 20 um, plain 180 mm lens. The front of the lens is 0.2-0.4 m from the
  tube wall. There is no in-air calibration.
- Target: a dot grid with a 3 mm pitch. The dot diameter is unsure (~2 mm).
- Traverse: 20 planes, `z_0mm` ... `z_38mm` in 2 mm steps. z = 0 is the plane farthest from the camera, and z grows
  towards it. The steps are not accurate.
- Tube axis: roughly along the dot columns. Its exact tilt and position are to be fitted.

**From a first look at the images** (rough threshold centroids, scratch scripts only)
- **Images:** 8 bit, with dark dots (~80) on a bright background (~115, noise sigma ~2). Each dot has a darker rim
  and horizontal streaks inside, like laser engraving. Its left and right edges are blurrier than its top and bottom,
  which is the tube's astigmatism. Each image holds 48-54 complete dots, and 9-14 more are cut by the border.
- **The tube magnifies only horizontally.** From z = 0 to 38 mm the column spacing falls from 155.7 to 141.0 px
  while the row spacing grows from 115.5 to 119.4 px, so the ratio goes from 1.35 to 1.18. A water cylinder
  predicts 1.33 at its axis and less towards the front wall, so the axis runs along the columns and the z = 0 plane
  sits a few mm behind it.
- **The extra column.** The leftmost column hardly moves (x = 45-47 px in every plane): it lies close to the plane
  through the camera and the tube axis. Because the horizontal spacing shrinks, a 9th complete column enters on the
  right from z = 14 mm. Indices will therefore be carried from plane to plane by tracking (dots move < 7 px per
  step against a 115 px pitch), never counted from the right edge.
- **The focal length is not 9000 px.** The row spacing grows only 3.4 % over the 38 mm traverse. Through the flat
  (axial) direction of the window that puts the centre of projection ~0.87 m (air-equivalent) from the board. With
  a 3 mm pitch the image scale (38.5 px/mm, magnification 0.77) then gives **f ~ 33,500 px**. A 180 mm macro lens at
  this magnification is telephoto-type: its entrance pupil, which is the pinhole model's centre, lies far behind
  the front element (pupil magnification ~0.3). That is consistent with the front being 0.2-0.4 m from the tube.
  - The alternative is that the stage moved only 10-17 mm instead of 38.
  - The tube itself measures the traverse: the horizontal magnification depends on the board's distance from the
    axis in units of the known radius. The fit with free plane positions (model M2) therefore decides between the
    two. A paraxial check already favours the nominal travel.
- **Consequences.**
  - The field of view is about ±1.1°, so the camera is nearly telecentric. The principal point cannot be separated
    from the rotation, and lens distortion is negligible over that angle.
  - All rays pass within ~25 mm of the axis. Every dot should have exactly one image, with no shadow or double
    images; test T6 checks this.

## 2. Frames and model

**World frame** = the board at z = 0.
- The origin is the dot in the leftmost (stationary) column, on the row nearest y = 318 px, which also barely moves.
- X runs along the rows (image right), Y along the columns (image up), Z towards the camera; the frame is
  right-handed.
- Dot (i, j) of plane k is at (3 i, 3 j, z_k) mm.
- The images might be mirrored by the camera software; if so this frame is mirrored too. That is harmless for one
  camera, but it has to be checked before a second camera shares the frame.

**Camera.** A pinhole in air:
- fx = fy = f is fitted, starting from a wide range;
- the principal point is fixed at the sensor centre (639.5, 399.5);
- there is no distortion;
- the pose (6) is fitted.

**Port.** pyOpenLPT's C++ `RefractionCylinderCameraModel` (PINCYL):
- radii [100.806, 97.631] mm and indices [1.000, 1.49, 1.333], all held at their nominal values;
- the axis line is fitted with 4 parameters: its direction near +Y (tilt within the board plane, and lean towards
  the camera) and its crossing of Y = 0 (X_a, Z_a).

**Planes.** z_k = 2k + dz_k with dz_0 = 0.

| model | free parameters | count |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | pose, f, axis; nominal z | 11 |
| **M2** (expected final) | M1 + 19 plane offsets dz_k | 30 |
| M3 | M2 + traverse direction (2 tilts), test only: probably degenerate with the camera pose in a telecentric view | 32 |

**Objective:** the reprojection error in pixels, using the C++ forward projection.
- The Jacobian is sparse finite-difference: a plane offset touches only its own plane, so all 19 cost one evaluation.
- There are two passes. The first is fitted plainly; then dots beyond 4 robust sigma (dirt on a dot) are flagged
  and dropped, and the model is refitted. Flagged dots are listed.
- Uncertainties come from the Jacobian at the solution (covariance and correlations), and also from
  leave-one-plane-out.

**Initialisation:** multi-start over the camera distance, the axis depth Z_a and f.
- The start for f follows from the measured vertical scale. Each start is fitted first with the fast
  back-projection residual (`lineOfSight`, ~2 ms per 1000 points). The best is then refined with forward
  projection.
- Everything is computed in millimetres and pixels, through the pyOpenLPT build in `build-py/Release` and the
  `refract` env.

## 3. Code (this folder)

| file | does |
|---|---|
| `config.json` | Every number of the case: paths, port, indices, pitch, planes, sensor. |
| `dots.py` | Detects the dots. The background comes from a morphological closing. Blobs cut by the border or not shaped like a dot are rejected. The sub-pixel centre is the centroid of the 50 % iso-contour (contourpy), which uses the outline and so ignores the rim and streaks. A second estimator, a saturated-weight centroid, is kept as a check. |
| `grid_index.py` | Estimates the lattice vectors per image, then carries the indices plane to plane by tracking. Checks: no duplicates, a complete rectangular block, the stationary column indexed 0 in every plane, and the new column appearing with the next index. |
| `cylfit.py` | Builds the PINCYL camera from a parameter vector. Holds the residuals (forward and back-projection), the multi-start, the sparse least squares, the outlier pass, the covariance, and save/load. |
| `run_calibration.py` | End to end: detection, indexing, fit, outputs. |
| `tests/` | T1-T7 below. Each script writes its output beside itself. |

Shared pyOpenLPT code is only imported (`pyopenlpt`, and the rotation helpers in `curved_calibration`). Nothing in
`modules/` or `src/` is changed. If a library bug blocks the work, I stop and report it first. Nothing is committed.

## 6. Risks and open points

- **The pitch sets the scale.** If it is not exactly 3 mm, every length scales with it. The tube can expose a gross
  error but cannot replace the pitch.
- **The near-telecentric view.** f, the camera distance and the traverse scale are strongly correlated. T3 and the
  covariance show whether the tube's ruler separates them. If it does not, I report the correlated combination
  instead of over-claiming.
- **Port values.** n_water and R1 are known to be nearly degenerate (E3 kit). They are held at nominal and their
  effect is budgeted in T7. Calipers on the outer diameter and the water temperature would tighten this.
- **Traverse tilt (M3).** A tilt cannot be told from the camera's pose in a telecentric view. The standard
  convention, a traverse perpendicular to the board, is kept unless T5 shows a clear, well-determined tilt.
- **Dot diameter.** It is measured from the images (needed only for T2 and the bias check in T6).
