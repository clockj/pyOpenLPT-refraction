## 3. Why the board's flatness decides it

- **f comes from perspective.** With the pupil 1.07 m away and a field of +-17 mm on the board, perspective is weak.
  A 45 deg tilt bends the image of a row of dots by only about 10 px at the edge of the field.
- **A bowed board bends it the same way.** Tilt a board by theta and give it a bow d = k x^2 / 2.
  - The bow changes the x^2 term of the image by the same factor sin(theta) as perspective does.
  - Reading that term as a flat board's gives f_app / f = 1 / (1 + k Z0 / (2 cos theta)).
  - In terms of the sag s at a distance rho from the board's centre: f_app / f - 1 = -s Z0 / (rho^2 cos theta + s Z0).
  - **A 5 um sag at 25 mm moves f by 1 %** (Z0 = 1,075 mm, theta = 30 deg).
- **The keystone tells them apart, weakly.** The keystone is the spacing across the tilt changing along it. It also
  carries perspective, and the bow leaves it unchanged. A fit can therefore separate the two, but only weakly.
- **Checked:** SymPy proves every step (13 PROVED; the 1 % is a numeric value). The check script joins the code in
  `tests/`.

**What the data cannot settle alone:**
- (a) whether the board really is bowed by about 40 um;
- (b) whether something else bends the image the same way: the detector under the defocus gradient of a tilted
  board, the lens's distortion, or the principal point.

The tests of section 5 address (b). For (a), I need either the board's flatness from you or a measurement that does
not depend on it.

**Recommended while the tube is out: a traverse in air.**
- **How:** the board square-on, on the stage, moved along the camera's axis in 0.1 in steps over about +-25 mm, as
  in the tube runs (about 20 positions). Leave the focus untouched.
- **What it gives:** the dot spacing against the stage position gives f directly. This is the tube report's check
  without the tube. The board's shape drops out, because it is the same at every position.
- **Expected precision:** about 0.2 %, as in the tube case.

If you take it, I would add its analysis (C1 in section 4).

## 4. What I propose to build and run

All the code goes in `cases/cyl_20260930_T0_cam1/focal/` (new). The outputs go to
`X:\Shijie Zhong\Refraction\Processed\20260930\T0\focal\`.

**A. Detection.**
- The case's `dots.py` gains one option, `background: "median"`, used for these images.
- Its default keeps the tube detections bit-identical (checked in F1).
- The fiducial indexing goes in `focal/fiducial_index.py`.
- `focal/focal_config.json` holds this run's numbers: paths, detector settings, fiducial sizes, pitch.

**B. The fit,** `focal/airfit.py`: a pinhole bundle adjustment.
- **Parameters:**
  - f, with fx = fy;
  - optionally the principal point and k1, and k2 or tangential terms as probes;
  - the 15 board poses;
  - optionally per-dot board corrections (the affine part held at the nominal grid, as in the tube case);
  - optionally a board bow (3 parameters).
- **Solver and statistics:** sparse Levenberg-Marquardt and the covariance.
- **Outliers:** one pass at 5 robust sigma, as in the tube case.
- **Cross-validation:** leave one image out, and the jackknife.

**C. The model choice,** with the rule fixed before the run:
- the lowest held-out RMS is chosen;
- models within 0.005 px of it tie, and a tie goes to fewer parameters;
- if F2 finds that the detector makes a fake bow larger than a third of the real one, the bow is not a candidate.

All models are reported. The result is stated twice: f for a flat board, and f with the bow fitted.

**D. Comparisons:** the comparison with the tube (F5) and the board's own errors (F6).

**C1 (only if you take the air traverse).** Two analyses:
- the spacing line;
- a pinhole fit to the planes at known z. It also goes through the route the GUI's plate calibration takes
  (`cv2.calibrateCamera` with all planes as one view), so your labmate can reproduce it in pyOpenLPT.

It would get its own short plan once the images exist.

**Why F6 matters for the tube calibration.**
- **What the tube fit found.** Its per-dot board corrections were 22.7 um RMS across the tube axis.
- **What would follow.** Suppose the board's own errors in air are only 6-8 um. Then most of those corrections were
  an image-fixed effect of the tube wall that the corrections absorbed (the board barely moved across the image
  during the traverse).
- **The consequence.** The delivered PINCYL camera does not carry that effect. Particles would then see about 1 px
  of error in u, not the 0.2 px the report gives.
- **Caveat.** The air images see a different part of the board, so I would state the conclusion with that caveat.
