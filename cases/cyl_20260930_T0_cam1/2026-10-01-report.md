### 2.2 The planes are held at the stage positions

The first run had to fit every plane's position, because 2 mm steps did not fit. With the true steps known, the plan
holds the planes at z = 2.54 k mm. The evidence for that choice:

- **The stage steps are regular.** The vertical dot spacing (along the tube axis) is linear in 2.54 k to 0.006 px
  RMS (section 3). The steps are therefore regular to about 0.07 mm or better.
- **Free plane positions absorb other errors.** Freed, the planes scatter by 0.17 mm per step. They then predict
  unseen planes at their stage positions much worse:
  - free planes (M2b): 0.51 px;
  - planes held (M1b): 0.34 px.
- **The first run's long traverse was a 1 % mismatch.** The first run's free planes came out 1.1 % longer (48.80 mm)
  than the true 48.26 mm. A free scale on the 2.54 mm steps prefers 1.0115 too (T5, D7), and the water index can
  do the same (section 6). In other words, the tube alone measured the traverse to 1.1 %.

### 2.3 The stage's sideways play, found and modelled

Holding the planes fixed (M1b, fit 0.303 px) exposed a per-plane pattern: all the dots of a plane share an offset.
`scratchpad/probe_perplane.py` measured it, and model M1bx now carries it.

- **Size:** the offsets are 0.20 px RMS in u and 0.12 px in v.
- **No trend, no memory:** they are independent from plane to plane (lag-1 correlation +0.07 and -0.01), with no
  trend in z.
- **Large share of the misfit:** removing them takes the residual from 0.30 to 0.20 px. A full affine map per
  plane removes only 0.004 px more.

This is the stage carriage moving the board sideways by a few micrometres at each step. M1bx gives each plane a
sideways shift (dX_k, dY_k) in the board plane, the block `shift` in `cylfit.py`. Two kinds of shift are left out:

- **A shift common to all planes:** it cannot be told from the camera's lateral position.
- **A shift linear in z:** it is the traverse tilt, which the data cannot fix (section 6).

That leaves 36 parameters. The fitted shifts are 4.0 / 3.2 um RMS, at most 9.3 um. The camera is unchanged by
them: M1b and M1bx project the calibrated volume to within 0.006 px of each other (D7).

## 3. The lens: why f is 41,500 px and not 9000

The user asked whether the 180 mm lens, focused by turning its ring, explains the large f. It does.

- **What the pinhole is.** The pinhole of the camera model is the lens's entrance pupil: the virtual image of the
  aperture seen from the front. Its distance from the object, together with the magnification, sets f.
- **What focusing does.** A 180 mm macro focused at infinity, with its pupil at the lens, would give
  f = 180 mm / 20 um = 9000 px. Focused close, at about 0.77x here, the lens moves its internal groups, and the
  pupil and the effective f move with them.

The data show where the pupil is without the tube model (`figures/dot_spacing.png`, `calibration_result.json` ->
`spacing_check`).

**Vertical spacing.** Along the tube axis, the wall acts as a flat window. The vertical dot spacing of the same 15
pairs grows from 115.37 to 119.40 px over the 48.26 mm traverse, +3.49 %.
- **A straight line, no tube model.** It is linear against z = 2.54 k to 0.006 px. A straight line through
  1/spacing gives f = 41,393 px and an air-equivalent distance of 1076 mm from the pupil to plane 0.
- **Where else the pinhole could be.** The photo's distances give the growth for other pupil positions:
  - pinhole at the lens front (20 cm from the wall): +14.5 %;
  - pinhole at the camera body (36.5 cm): +8.7 %.

  The calibrated model gives +3.51 %.
- **f held at 9000 px fits 8.4 px RMS, 43 times worse (T5).**

**Horizontal spacing.** Across the axis, the tube's curvature shrinks the horizontal spacing by 9.9 % over the
traverse, and the model gives 10.3 %. The 0.4 % difference is the main remaining model error (section 6).

So the pupil is about 1.1 m from the board, roughly 60 cm behind the camera body. This is the near-telecentric
behaviour of a long macro lens at close focus. **The calibration holds for this focus setting only**: turning the
focus ring moves the pupil and changes f.

### 5.1 T4: why it fails, and what it shows

**The criterion and the failure.** The plan's criterion is that the error at the stage position be below 1.5x the
training rms, and likewise with the plane's own z. The own-z part passes (1.44, 1.43). The stage-position part
fails (1.76, 1.72).

**Why.** The two errors are not alike. M1bx's training rms has each training plane's sideways play fitted out. An
unseen plane's play cannot be predicted, so its error at the stage position includes about 0.2 px of play.

**The like-for-like comparison.** With the unseen plane's sideways shift fitted (2 parameters, the same nuisance as
in training), the ratios are 1.03 and 1.06.

**M1b.** M1b passes the same criterion (0.343 against 0.300) only because its training rms includes the play. It
predicts unseen planes no better than M1bx: 0.343 against 0.342 px. I did not change the criterion. The failure
says that the stage-position error of an unseen plane is dominated by that plane's own play.

**Other findings from T4.**
- The 20 leave-one-out fits agree on f to 13.5 px and on the camera centre to (0.08, 0.06, 0.35) mm.
- The largest error at a stage position, 0.60 px, is plane 5.08 mm, the plane with the largest play (9.3 um).

## 6. Accuracy, and what the data do not determine

**1. The model error is largest at the ends of the traverse.**
- The per-plane residual runs from 0.31 px (z = 0) through 0.07 px (z = 25.4 mm) to 0.34 px (z = 48.26 mm), mostly
  in u.
- Unseen planes with their own shift fitted show the same shape: 0.33, 0.07 and 0.36 px.
- The pattern changes sign between the near and far planes, as an image-fixed deflection at the tube wall would
  after the per-dot board corrections have absorbed its average. The first run found the same (D1-D5).
- The diagnostic wall field (M1bxw) cuts the own-shift error from 0.20 to 0.13 px. PINCYL cannot carry it.
- This error will grow outside the calibrated planes.

**2. The water's index (T7).**
- With the planes fixed, the fit improves from 0.196 to 0.191 px at n_water = 1.3376. A parabola through the scan
  gives a minimum at 1.3376, with a formal width of 0.0005 at least. That is water at about 475 nm (blue), or a
  tube slightly smaller than nominal.
- Per +0.002 in the index, f moves by -255 px, the camera by 6.4 mm in Z, and the axis by 0.7 mm in X.
- Points in the calibrated volume project within 0.055 px RMS (max 0.21) either way. Through the full tube depth
  (z -80..100 mm), the difference is 0.34 px RMS, at most 2.2 px (D7).
- The index is not fitted. Its colour dependence makes the light source the better source of the value.

**3. The other tube constants (T7).**
- n_acrylic +-0.01: f moves by 24 px.
- R_outer +-0.25 mm: f moves by 91-97 px, and the axis by 0.4 mm in X.
- Wall thickness +-0.2 mm: f moves by 27 px.
- n_air 1.00027: f moves by 45 px.
- None of them changes the fit by more than 0.002 px.

**4. The stage scale.** The data prefer steps 1.1 % longer (fit 0.005 px better, T5, D7).
- Together with item 2, this is the 1.1 % mismatch the first run saw. It sits between the stage's steps and the
  tube's refraction.
- Applied, it would move predictions by 0.45 px in the calibrated volume. Most of that is the z coordinate itself
  rescaled, i.e. the world frame, not the camera.
- The planes stay at 2.54 mm, as the user specified.

**5. The traverse direction.**
- The data cannot tell a board moving along its normal from one moving 6 deg off it. The fit with the traverse
  tilt is 0.004 px better (T5, D7).
- The tilted fit moves the camera 121 mm sideways and turns its rays in the board frame by 6.2 deg. Its world
  frame is sheared by tau = -0.108 mm per mm of z.
- The delivered calibration assumes no tilt. If the board was not square to the rail, positions reconstructed in
  this frame are sheared in the same way (X off by 0.108 z, 5 mm at z = 48 mm).
- This is a property of the target's motion, which no camera fit can see. Checking the board's squareness to the
  rail settles it.

**6. The lateral geometry is now well determined, given the frame.**
- In the first run (free planes), the axis's lateral position ranged over 2-19 mm between models. With the planes
  held, it is 20.87 +- 0.14 mm. Near-equal fits put it at 19.3-20.9 mm (n_water, stage scale, M2b).
- Holding it 5 mm off costs 0.04-0.07 px of fit, against 0.005 px for those alternatives. It changes predictions by
  0.18-0.22 px in the calibrated volume and 0.9-1.0 px through the tube depth (D7, probes).

**Realistic accuracy for particles.**
- **Inside the calibrated volume:** about 0.2 px RMS (4-5 um at the object). That is 0.07 px at mid-traverse,
  0.3-0.36 px at the ends.
- **Outside it:** worse, by roughly 0.3-1 px RMS through the full depth of the tube, from the index alone and the
  model error.
- **On top of either:** the frame's own uncertainty (items 4 and 5).

## 7. Changes from the plan

1. **The plane-shift block, new.** The plan expected M1b. M1bx was added after the M1b residuals showed the
   per-plane offsets (section 2.3), and T5's rule selected it.
2. **T5's choice rule.** The tie margin and the k1 exclusion (section 2.4) were added after the first T5 output
   picked M1bk. Both are written into T5's docstring. Separately, the column/row board start (M1c) was fixed and T5
   rerun.
3. **T3's criterion for the plane shifts**, a new block: z-score RMS between 0.5 and 1.5 and none beyond 4. For 120
   values (108 independent), a "below 3 each" rule would fail about one run in four by chance. The camera and port
   parameters keep the "below 3" rule.
4. **T4 fails as specified** (section 5.1). The criterion was not changed.
5. **D7's set of fits** was revised after its first run:
   - added the refits with the preferred water index and a free stage scale;
   - kept the axis +-5 mm, wall field and traverse tilt as labelled probes (their caveats are in the docstring).
6. **T1b was not rerun** (inputs unchanged).
7. **T6 was rerun** on the camera file of the final output rewrite (same result).
8. **Two outliers.** For the first time the approved 5-robust-sigma pass dropped two dots, both in column 6 of the
   two nearest planes, with 0.4-0.6 px residuals. They are not bad detections but the end-plane model error at
   its largest.
9. **Sign of the residuals in the outputs (fixed after the first delivery).** The dot tables' `res_u, res_v`, the
   residual-vector figure and the per-plane means in `calibration_result.json` now all give detected minus model,
   as the overlays always drew them. Before, the figure (titled "detected minus model") and the tables' used dots
   carried model minus detected, while the tables' outlier rows carried detected minus model. Fit and camera are
   unchanged.

## 8. Recommendations

1. **Do not touch the focus ring** between this calibration and the experiment. Recalibrate after any refocus.
2. **Check that the board is square to the rail.** The world frame assumes it (section 6, item 5).
3. **Note the light's colour,** or put a filter on the light. It sets the water's index, which moves the absolute
   geometry (section 6, item 2).
4. **Calibrate over the depth you measure in.** These planes cover z = 0..48 mm, 13 mm behind to 35 mm in front of
   the axis. The tube in view spans about -85..+110 mm.
5. **More stage travel with the same steps would also help:** it extends the volume and strengthens the f /
   distance determination.
