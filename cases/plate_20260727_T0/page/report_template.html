The flat-window calibration on your tank rig

Four Phantom cameras behind two perpendicular acrylic walls, and 10,936 frames of a 10 mm wand. The calibration works, and it is 6–8× better than your GUI calibration of the same data. Three things were needed: two library fixes, fitting the lens distortion, and a start from a verified answer, because from scratch the fit can land in a wrong one. Your channel's size then showed the C3/C4 wall placed 9 cm too close. A second calibration holds the channel, with each lens's centre kept at the image centre as you prefer. It puts the walls in place, just as accurately.

Terms. Every calibration is judged the same way. Each frame's two balls are triangulated from all four cameras, without the wand length. Reprojection error: how far each detection lies from its triangulated ball's image. Wand error: the triangulated length minus 10 mm. Right answer: the one every verified fit agrees on, with the walls 90.2° apart and the cameras 0.7–1.1 m from their walls.

In short

Nine results, most important first

  1. Use the calibration with the channel, calib_plate_wand_ref_centre. The first calibration put the C3/C4 wall 9 cm too close to its cameras, so the wand reached 225 mm into your 150 mm channel. The new one holds the channel, keeps each lens's centre at the image centre as you prefer, and puts the wand 16–132 mm from that wall. It is as accurate as the first: 0.218 px and 0.0068 mm. A variant that also fits the lens centres, calib_plate_wand_ref, is 3–4 % better (next section).
  2. The calibration works and beats the GUI's. On every frame, its median reprojection error is 6× lower and its median wand error 8× lower. On calib_3's own filtered frames it is 4× and 7× better. The wand comes out at 9.9998 mm on average, with an SD of 0.010 mm.
  3. Two library bugs had to be fixed first, with your approval. One was a crash in the start-up stage, shared with the cylinder calibration. The other was a camFile check too strict for rounding. Both have regression tests, and every test suite passes.
  4. The lens distortion must be fitted (k1). It is about 6 px at the image corners. The water index and the wall thickness, within ±0.003 and ±0.3 mm, make no measurable difference.
  5. The real focal lengths are 18,750–21,940 px, not 13,333. calib_3 held f at the nominal value, which put its cameras about 30 % too close to each other and to the walls.
  6. From scratch, the calibration can end in a wrong answer on this rig, as it did on 2 of 9 frame sets. C3 and C4 see the wand under only about 6°, the limit where the synthetic tests also failed. The report's warnings missed 2 of the 4 wrong answers.
  7. A start from a verified answer, or rough hints, avoids it. The delivered fit was refitted on every frame from the first half of the recording, in 5 minutes.
  8. The report's “pinned” warning hid how freely the walls move. A few frames where the wand touched the C1/C2 wall, with its ball cut by C3's or C4's image border, hold the fit. That costs 0.5 % in C1's and C2's f. It also made the walls look firmly placed. Holding each ball its own radius inside the walls frees the fit.
  9. The wand alone places the walls only to several cm. Your channel fixes that, and measurements inside the wand's volume do not change either way. The cameras did not drift during the recording.

Your channel

The C3/C4 wall was 9 cm off; held by the channel, it is in place

Your channel is 15 × 10 cm inside, and C1 and C2 look through its long side. So the wand must stay within 100 mm of the C1/C2 wall and 150 mm of the C3/C4 wall. The first calibration had it 0–85 mm from the C1/C2 wall, which fits, but 109–225 mm from the C3/C4 wall, which cannot. C1 and C2 aimed 168 mm along their wall, outside the channel, while C3 and C4 aimed at the middle of theirs.

  • Why: the wand alone places a wall only through a faint refraction effect, a few tenths of a pixel at the image edges. Two things in the fit pulled wall 2 off. The fit was pinned against wall 1: a wand end sat on its surface, so every step was refused. And each lens's centre, the principal point, was held at the image centre. Freed of both, the data alone move wall 2 about 70 mm towards the channel.
  • The fix: two optional settings in plate_wand, both off by default. The first holds constraints. The channel: every wand end stays inside it, each ball at least its radius from every wall. The aim: C1 and C2 look at the middle of the long side, as you said they roughly do. The second fits lens terms, such as the principal point.
  • Two results are in the table. With the lens centres held at the image centre, your choice, the fit is as good as the first calibration's. With them fitted, it is 3–4 % better, on frames it did not use too. With the centres fitted, C3 and C4 also aim at the middle of their wall (47 of 100 mm) and the wand touches the C1/C2 wall, as the data hinted earlier. With the centres held, they aim at 62 mm, and the C1/C2 wall sits 13 mm further from the wand.
  • From scratch, with the channel held, the calibration found the right answer on every frame, with and without the principal point. Without the channel, the same data had ended in a wrong one.

The main thing to watch

From scratch, the fit can land in a wrong answer

Wall 2 is seen only by C3 and C4, which see the wand under about 6° with their real focal lengths. A wall's tilt then changes their images only slightly, and the fit has a second, wrong minimum. Which minimum the calibration reaches depends on the frames it starts from.

  • Kind A: both walls are wrong. They come out 93.5–94° apart, with every camera 0.2–0.3 m from its wall. This happened on every frame (T2's first run), on subset s0 with k1 held at 0, and on s0 with the edge filter.
  • Kind B: only wall 2 is wrong, tilted the other way. C3 and C4 swap their angles to it and sit 0.2 m from it, while the walls stay 90.1° apart. This happened on the second half of the recording, with k1 fitted.

The “pinned” warning and the walls

A few frames hold wall 1; the measurement does not care

Most fits end with the report's pinned warning: a wand end lies exactly on wall 1's water side.

  • The frames: they are frames where the wand touched or nearly touched the C1/C2 wall. In each, the near-wall ball is cut by C3's or C4's left image border, which biases its centre. 697 detections in all have a ball cut by the border.
  • The fit stops at the wall. A step that would push those ends into the acrylic is refused. Holding the wall 2–5 mm back and refitting lowers the cost. In the pinned fits, C1's and C2's f come out about 0.5 % low, and wall 1 2–5 mm off.
  • The measurement does not change. Dropping the border-cut detections frees the pin but leaves the wand error unchanged: 0.0070 against 0.0067 mm on unused frames.
  • The wand alone places the walls only to several cm. Their positions come from the third-order refraction effect, strongest at the image edges where detections are least reliable. They moved 20–40 mm between frame sets, and the pin made them look firmer than they are. Moving a wall by ±10–20 mm and refitting leaves the wand error at 0.0066–0.0069 mm.
  • The channel setting frees the pin. It holds each ball at least its radius inside every wall, so a touching wand end no longer blocks the fit. With the channel and the aim, the walls are where your channel puts them: the wand is 5–73 mm from the C1/C2 wall and 26–127 mm from the C3/C4 wall, 76 mm high (98 % of the wand ends).

What else was tested

Repeatable, no drift, and only k1 among the inputs matters

Repeatability and held-out frames

  • Subsets: the five interleaved subsets (every 5th frame) agree. The walls angle is 90.157 ± 0.023°, the baselines agree to 0.1–3 mm, and C3's and C4's f vary less than their own error bars.
  • No over-fitting: each fit predicts the 80 % of frames it never saw as well as its own. The median wand error there is 0.0066–0.0069 mm, with an SD of 0.010 mm.
  • No drift: the two halves of the recording agree. f agrees within 0.05–0.9σ for C1–C3 and within 0.3 % for C4, and only wall 2's position moves, by about 20 mm.

Library fixes

Two bugs that only real data showed

  • cylinder_wand.affine_factorization, stage 1 of both wand calibrations, crashed with an IndexError on more than about 550 frames. Gross detection errors kept its outlier rejection going to the last round, and it then returned frames it had not factorised. It now returns the ones it used, so every run that worked before gives the same result.
  • plate_model.to_cpp refused to write two cameras' camFiles, because the C++ side rebuilt their rotation 1.2e-14 off. Its check is now 1e-12, still seven orders of magnitude below the errors it was written to catch.
  • Checks: each fix has a regression test in tests/test_plate_wand.py. Every suite passed: plate 14 fast + 4 slow, cylinder 6 + 1. You committed both (180063f).
  • New options, for your channel (approved 3 Oct): plate_wand.calibrate(fit_lens=...) fits the principal point, k2 or the tangential terms. calibrate(constraints=...) holds a channel the wand stays in, where cameras aim, or a camera's distance to its wall. Both are off by default, so earlier results are unchanged. Three new regression tests; the plate suite passes, 21 tests with the slow ones. Not committed.

For the experiment

Using this calibration, and the next one

Use the calibration with the channel

  • The camFiles are calib_plate_wand_ref_centre\camFile\cam0.txt … cam3.txt, for C1 … C4, named as in calib_3.
  • Tape-measure the C1–C2 and C3–C4 spacings: 243 and 288 mm.
  • Distances to the C3/C4 wall rest on C1 and C2 aiming at the channel's middle.

Calibrate this rig again

  • Fit k1: --fit-k1. Add --fit-lens pp to fit the lens centres too.
  • Hold the channel, and the aim if you know it: --channel --aim centre.
  • Start from this calibration, or give window-normal hints.
  • Check the result against the tables above, not only the warnings.

Record the wand

  • Keep both balls fully inside every image.
  • Touch each wall with the wand for a second or two, the ball fully in view. With the channel held, that pins the wall to a millimetre or two.
  • Give C3 and C4 a wider view: move the wand over more of their images, or use a shorter lens.

Software, your call

  • Make the start more robust: when it joins the two camera pairs, it can begin 30 px off.
  • Choose between starts by cost, not by detections kept.
  • Free a wall pinned by a few frames: done by the channel's ball radii; without a channel it remains.
  • Flag a noise twice the best start's, beyond the fixed 0.5 px.
