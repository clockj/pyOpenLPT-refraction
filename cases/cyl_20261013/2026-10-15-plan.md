### How each setting is chosen

These rules work for any new recording.
1. **Measure the balls first.** Use Test Detect on a few frames where the wand is broadside to every camera, and read
   each camera's two radii.
2. **Radius Range:**
   - **The rule:** R min ≈ 0.6 × the small ball's radius, R max ≈ 1.5 × the large ball's.
   - **What it does:** it sets the blob-size filter (radii from R min/2 to 1.5 R max) and the background window
     (2.5 × R max, which must be wider than a ball).
   - **Why not tighter:** too small an R max loses frames. On 6 Oct's T2 (1,200 frames), 20–60 px paired 108 frames
     where 40–120 px paired 159.
3. **Rod opening:**
   - **The rule:** wider than the rod's image, narrower than the small ball's.
   - **How to find it:** on Test Detect, raise it until the rod disappears and the two balls are separate blobs.
     Stop before the small ball vanishes, and take the middle of the range that works.
   - **This run:** the range is narrow, because the 2 mm rod is 2/3 of the 3 mm ball. That is why each camera gets
     its own value: cam2 needs 57 px where cam1 works at 51.
4. **Rod mask:**
   - **The rule:** the rod's half-width plus about twice its blur, about 0.55–0.6 × the opening.
   - **What it does:** it leaves out the edge points where the rod joins the ball.
5. **Per camera rather than shared:** cam3's pixels are 1.48 × smaller, so its balls are about 1.5 × larger.
   - **Shared settings:** with the Pixel column, they would scale cam1's opening 51 to 76 px for cam3.
   - **What works on cam3:** 85–89 px.
6. **Sensitivity** is not used in the ellipse mode (it is greyed out).

## 6. A known limit

- **f is not fixed by the wand alone.** The cameras are far from the tube with long lenses, so the wand fixes each
  camera's f divided by its distance but not f itself. On 6–8 Oct, parts of a recording gave f values ±10 % apart.
- **Tracking is not affected.** Inside the wand's volume the cameras still agree, which is what tracking needs.
- **To pin f:** a dot board imaged through the tube, or a "hold f" option in the calibration (not built).
  - **Corrected 15 Oct:** the option exists (`fix_f`, built on 7 Oct, T14), in the library but not in the GUI's
    panel. What is missing is a reliable f: on 7 Oct the dot board gave only cam2's f, to about ±3 %.
