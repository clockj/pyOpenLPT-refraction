## 3. The settings, and how to choose them

**In the GUI** (Calibration → Wand Calibration, Pinhole+Cylinder):

| control | value |
|---|---|
| Num Cameras, images | 3; the cam1, cam2, cam3 folders, every one from `frame000000` |
| Pixel (µm) | 20, 20, 13.5 |
| Wand Length | 10 mm |
| Detection mode, Wand Type | ellipse; Dark on Bright |
| Different settings for each camera | ticked: the rows below |
| Tube Settings | the defaults (Air / Acrylic 1.49 / Water 1.333; OD 201.6125 mm; wall 3.175 mm) |
| Noise weights; start search; fit wand length | Per camera; normal; off |

| camera | R min | R max | Opening | Mask |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cam1 | 20 | 70 | 51 | 30 |
| cam2 | 20 | 70 | 57 | 30 |
| cam3 | 30 | 105 | 85 | 44 |

**How each was chosen** (the same steps work for a new recording):
1. **Measure each camera's two ball radii.** Use Test Detect on a frame where the wand is broadside to every camera.
2. **Radius Range:** R min ≈ 0.6 × the small radius, R max ≈ 1.5 × the large one.
   - **What it does:** it sets the blob-size filter (radii R min/2 to 1.5 R max) and the background window
     (2.5 × R max).
   - **Too small an R max loses frames:** on 6 Oct's T2, 20–60 px paired 108 frames where 40–120 px paired 159.
3. **Rod opening:** wider than the rod's image, narrower than the small ball's.
   - **How:** raise it until the rod disappears, stop before the small ball does, and take the middle.
   - **Here:** the window is narrow and differs per camera (fine scan on 24 frames, `tests/p0d_wand_survey_scanfine_out.txt`):
     - cam1 works at 49–55 px;
     - cam2 needs about 57 px;
     - cam3 85–89 px (8 Oct's 77 px separated 16 of 23 frames, 85 px 21).
4. **Rod mask:** the rod's half-width plus about twice its blur, about 0.55–0.6 × the opening.
5. **Per-camera rows, not shared ones:** scaling the shared settings by the pixel sizes would give cam3 an opening of
   76 px.
6. **Most lost camera-frames are not detector failures** (`tests/p0e_failures.png`): the wand seen end-on (its balls
   overlap) or outside a camera's view. "One ball" and "no ball" together are 35 %, 29 % and 18 % of the
   camera-frames.

## 6. What this means

- **The calibration is usable for tracking, with a looser tolerance than 8 Oct's.** Three-camera matching needs
  about 2.5–3 px (8 Oct: 2.0 px, which held 89 % of the true matches). The held-out errors' 95th percentiles are
  2.9–5.2 px.
- **What makes this run noisier is not in the settings.** Both balls' centres are 30–50 % noisier in every camera
  than on 8 Oct. Section 10 splits this by camera: the wand's path for cam2, the detections for cams 1 and 3.
  - **cam3's ellipse fits are noisier too:** edge RMS median 0.25 px against 0.18 px on 8 Oct (the surveys).
    Its images changed: focus or light.
  - **Cams 1–2's edge RMS is 8 Oct's** (0.17 and 0.15 against 0.18 and 0.16 px), so their extra noise is not in the
    fits.
  - **What remains:** the wand's path through more of the tube, where the model or the ellipse centre is less exact.
    The residuals grow where the tube stretches the balls most.
  - **Not tested:** a comparison of the balls' edge sharpness between the runs would tell whether cam3's focus
    changed.
- **f stays undetermined.**
  - **The option exists:** the calibration can hold f (`fix_f`, built and tested on 7 Oct, T14), from the library
    but not from the GUI's panel.
  - **What is missing is a reliable f to hold.** On 7 Oct a dot board imaged through the tube gave only cam2's f, to
    about ±3 %.

## 9. Your follow-up: only the frames all three cameras see (V2)

You asked: "what if you only use the frames that all cameras can see for calibration? will that reduce the error?"

**The test** (`tests/v2_all_three.py`, `tests/v2b_wand_length_same_frames.py`): C1 refitted on the 5,596 frames all
three cameras keep. This is the GUI's own rule, which keeps a frame only when every camera has a pair. It is compared
with C1, which used 9,139 frames, on the same measures.

| measure | C1 (at least two cameras) | all three only |
|---|---|---|
| frames used | 9,139 | 5,596 |
| fit RMS | 0.980 px (1.020 / 0.637 / 1.157) | 0.959 px (0.975 / 0.671 / 1.166) |
| each camera predicted from the other two (5,050 frames) | 1.517 / 2.695 / 2.278 px | 1.515 / 2.676 / 2.271 px |
| wand length on the 5,593 frames both used (all three cameras) | 44.9 µm RMS | 44.6 µm RMS |
| wand length on the 3,543 two-camera frames (all3 never saw them) | 102.8 µm RMS | 103.2 µm RMS |
| f | 16,198 / 19,849 / 26,130 px | 16,170 / 19,911 / 26,398 px |

**No, it does not reduce the error.**
- **Every measure changes by 1 % or less.** The fit RMS falls by 2 %, but per camera it goes both ways: cam1 down,
  cams 2–3 up. Its fall comes from which frames are counted, not from better cameras.
- **The wand-length figures need care.** The earlier 72 µm against 45 µm compares different frames.
  - **On the same three-camera frames** both fits give 45 µm.
  - **On the two-camera frames** both give about 100 µm.
  - **By pair:** cams 1 and 3, almost one above the other, give 128 µm there. This is a geometry limit of seeing
    with two cameras, not a calibration error, so dropping those frames from the fit does not change it.
- **The two-camera frames neither help nor hurt this calibration,** because the three-camera frames fix the cameras.
  - **The GUI's rule gives the same calibration:** fitting in the GUI with these settings should come out the same.
  - **C1 stays the delivered one.** It covers more of each camera's view at no cost.
- **What would reduce the error** lies upstream of the fit: the detections. Both balls are 30–50 % noisier than on
  8 Oct, and cam3's fits are noisier than its own were (section 6).

## 10. Your follow-up: is it focus? (D2)

You asked: "so the main reason is that this experiment does not focus the wand points well, right?"

**The test** (`tests/d2_focus_or_path.py`, read-only, both runs' delivered fits). Every used detection is placed by
three things:
- its ball's stretch (the aspect ratio, which depends on where the ball is in the tube);
- its distance from the tube's axis;
- its depth along the camera's view.

Then two questions:
- **Path:** does today's mix of places, at 8 Oct's error per place, reproduce today's error?
- **Detections:** is today worse at the same place?

| camera | RMS 8 Oct → 13 Oct | explained by the mix of stretch / distance / depth | at the same stretch, today against 8 Oct |
|---|---|---|---|
| cam1 | 0.742 → 1.020 px | 18 % / 9 % / −1 % | worse in every bin (e.g. aspect 1.25–1.30: 0.63 → 1.06 px) |
| cam2 | 0.531 → 0.637 px | **106 %** / 14 % / −3 % | the same or better (1.30–1.35: 0.68 → 0.63 px) |
| cam3 | 0.780 → 1.157 px | 21 % / 14 % / 0 % | worse in every bin (1.30–1.35: 0.84 → 1.27 px) |

**Not mainly focus; it differs by camera.**
- **cam2: the wand's path.** Its balls were more stretched today (median aspect 1.30 against 1.23). At the same
  stretch it detects as well as on 8 Oct. Its whole rise comes from where the wand went.
- **cam3: its detections.** It is noisier at the same stretch, distance and depth, and its ellipse outlines are
  rougher (edge scatter 0.25 against 0.18 px). This fits focus or lighting.
- **cam1: its detections, but not plain blur.** It is noisier at the same place too, but its outlines are as clean
  as on 8 Oct (0.17 against 0.18 px). Something moves the whole ellipse without roughening it.
  - **The candidates:** blur that differs between the horizontal and the vertical (the tube separates the two foci),
    or uneven backlight.
  - **A hint:** cam1's aspect rose from 1.32 to 1.39, more than the other cameras'.
- **Depth of field is not it.** Today's wand went through less depth than 8 Oct's, and the error does not grow with
  depth more than it did then.
- **Not tested:** the images themselves. Comparing the balls' edge profiles between the runs, horizontally and
  vertically, would show whether cam3 is defocused and whether cam1's blur differs between the two directions.
