## 1. What the old run shows

**The old run's inputs:**
- **camFiles:** `camFile_VSC` (the GUI's calibration after VSC).
  - f is 13,333 px, with no lens distortion.
  - Their header gives a reprojection error of 1.64 ± 1.01 px.
  - Projection tolerance 1e-6, at most 50 iterations.
- **`config.txt`:**
  - view volume x [-40, 65], y [-45, 30], z [-45, 35] mm in the old calibration's frame;
  - 0.105 mm per voxel, 26 threads.
- **`tracerConfig.txt`:**
  - IPR: 2 loops on all four cameras, at 2 then 3 px;
  - then each 3-camera set, 2 loops at 2 and 3 px;
  - 3-D tolerance 1 voxel.

**The logs:**
- `log_0_1500.txt`: frames 0–1740, from scratch. It ran an earlier tracerConfig: 4 loops on all four cameras, at 2,
  3, 4.5 and 6.75 px, and no 3-camera sets.
- `log.txt`: frames 3501–4687, run with the current tracerConfig and started from the tracks saved at frame 3500.

**What they show:**
1. **Matching on all four cameras never works at 2 px.** It found 0 particles in every frame of both logs, and only
   9–48 at 3 px.
   - In `log_0_1500` most particles came at 4.5 and 6.75 px.
   - In `log.txt` they came from the 3-camera sets: [0,1,3] found the most; [0,2,3] and [1,2,3] found almost none at 2 px.
2. **The time per frame grows during the run.**
   - `log.txt`: from 6 s at frame 3501 to 110 s at frame 4686.
   - Almost all of it is the matching of one 3-camera set, [0,2,3], which went from 1.6 s to 78 s per frame. Its
     detection counts stayed the same (about 14k, 18k and 37k).
   - `log_0_1500`: from 5 s at frame 100 to 28 s at frame 1600.
3. **Saving the tracks every 500 frames** took 2–4 min in `log_0_1500` and 18–20 min in `log.txt` (track files of
   230–410 MB written to X:).
4. **Tracks die fast.**
   - Per frame, about 390 (`log_0_1500`) and 600–650 (`log.txt`) long tracks failed the linear-fit test, out of
     8,600–9,700 active: 4.5–7 % of the long tracks per frame.
   - About as many new long tracks were made each frame.

## 2. What changes with the new camFiles

- **The calibration itself:**
  - f is 18,784–22,033 px and k1 is 0.81–1.01.
  - RMS 0.28 px on the wand, and 0.218 px median on every frame of the wand table.
- **Another world frame.**
  - Origin at the wand's mean position, Z = the C1/C2 wall's normal, Y = up.
  - The old frame differs by about a half-turn about the vertical, and its lengths are about 30 % short (f 13,333 against
    about 19,000–22,000 px).
  - So the old view volume and voxel size cannot be reused as they are. T0 maps them.
- **Projection tolerance 1e-12, at most 1000 iterations** (the old files: 1e-6 and 50). The flat-window projection
  iterates, and matching calls it millions of times per frame, so T1 checks what this costs.
- **The linear-fit test is a fixed 0.05 mm** (`MAX_ERR_LINEARFIT` in `inc/libMath/STBCommons.h`, not in the config).
  In true-scale coordinates it is about 1.3–1.4× stricter, physically, than it was in the old frame.

## 3. The code

- **The old run** used openlpt 2.2.4, built on 13 Jul from `D:\Bubble_Reconstruct\OpenLPT_GUI` (env
  `bubble-visual-hull`).
- **Its tracking code is the same as pyOpenLPT's today:** STB, IPR, StereoMatch, Shake, PredField, OTF, Config and
  ObjectFinder are identical files.
- **`Camera.cpp` differs** only in the curved-window cameras, the camFile's printed precision and the handling of
  negative distortion coefficients. None of this touches these camFiles: the old ones have no distortion, the new ones a
  positive k1.
- **Every test runs pyOpenLPT's current build** (`build-py/Release`, env `refract`) for both camera sets, unchanged.

**Something to measure, not to change:**
- To find candidates, matching draws each line of sight in the other cameras as a straight 2-D line through the images
  of two points 1 and 2 mm past its wall (`StereoMatch::makeLine2DFromLOS3D`).
- Through a flat window that image is a curve.
- So the line can miss a true match by more than the 2 px tolerance far from that wall, even with a perfect
  calibration. T2 measures how far.

## 4. Tests

| test | what | judged by |
|---|---|---|
| T0 frame and config | Project a grid filling the old view volume with the old cameras, triangulate those pixels with the new cameras, and fit a similarity (scale s, rotation, shift). The old box mapped this way gives the new view volume, clipped to the channel's water (walls from the new camFiles; inside 100 × 150 mm). The voxel size becomes 0.105 × s mm, so the search radii, shake width and 3-D tolerance keep the physical size they had. Also: how much of the box each camera sees, and how many cameras see it. | the mapping's residual is small against the voxel size; the box lies in the water and in the cameras' views |
| T1 projection cost | Every new camera projects 100k points of the new view volume, with the delivered tolerance (1e-12 / 1000) and with the old files' (1e-6 / 50). Measure the time per call, the failures and the pixel difference. Also the old cameras, and the cost of `lineOfSight`. | if 1e-12 costs over 1.5× the time, or fails over 0.1 % of the points, T4 also runs a short variant with 1e-6 / 50 |
| T2 epipolar lines | For every camera pair, take points across the view volume. Find each point's distance in camera j from the 2-D line that matching builds from camera i's line of sight. Done for both camera sets, and for a line through two points inside the view volume, for comparison. | the share of true matches farther than 2 and 3 px from their line |
| T3 IPR on raw images | One IPR, with no tracking, on frames 0, 1000, 2000, 3000, 3600 and 4600, with each camera set. Measure the time and the particles found per camera set and tolerance. It needs no tracking state, so it also shows the late frames where the old run became slow. | particles found on all four cameras at 2 px; the matching time |
| T4 STB, new camFiles | Frames 0–1000 from scratch, with the current tracerConfig and the T0 config. Writes the full log, the tracks saved at frames 500 and 1000, and the wall time. | speed and track removal, against T5 |
| T5 STB, old camFiles | The same frames, build and tracerConfig, with `camFile_VSC` and the old view volume: the control, under the same conditions as T4. | — |
| T6 comparison | Parse both logs into per-frame tables and figures. Speed: time per frame (with and without the frames that save), matching time per camera set. Tracks: active long tracks, linear-fit and shaking failures per frame as a share of the active long tracks, and the length of the long tracks in the saved files. Particles: found per frame. The original logs are added for context. | the final report |

**Order:** T0–T3 first (about 30 min), then T4 and T5 one after the other, so that they do not compete for the cores.

**Timing noise:** other programs on this machine can make wall times vary by 10–20 %. Each comparison is therefore run
under the same conditions, and the logs' counts (detections, matched particles, tracks) are compared besides the
times.

**Expected time:**
- T4: about 1–3 h.
- T5: about 3–5 h, judging from the old logs' early frames.

If T5 runs slower than about 20 s per frame, I stop it at frame 500 and compare frames 0–500.

## 6a. Additions during the run (4 Oct)

- **Resource sampling** in `run_stb.py`: `resources.csv` beside each log, every 15 s. It records the process's memory
  and CPU use and the other programs' CPU use (Windows API through ctypes; `psutil` is not in `refract` and I did not
  install it).
  - **Why:** T3 found that on raw images a frame costs the same at frame 4600 as at frame 0, so the old run's slowdown
    builds up inside the running process.
- **T6b, track diagnostics** (`tests/t6b_track_noise.py`), from the saved tracks:
  - the particles' speed;
  - their position noise;
  - the linear-fit residual of the points that passed and of the points that ended a track.
  - **Why:** T4's first frames remove about 1,850 long tracks per frame by the linear-fit test. The question is
    whether these are noisy real particles or ghosts.
- **T1's rule did not trigger:** the 1e-12 tolerance costs nothing measurable, so there is no T4 variant.
- **T4c, restart test** (`tests/t4c_restart.py`): a fresh process loads T4's active tracks at frame 500 and runs
  frames 501–600 (about 15 min, after T4).
  - **Why:** in T4 the matching of [0,2,3] slows down from about frame 70 (1.2 s, then 4.8 s by frame 164), while the
    detections fall. The old `log.txt` run, started from saved tracks at frame 3500, was fast at first and slowed down
    over about 500 frames.
  - **The question:** is it the tracking state or the age of the process?
  - **First frames (501–505):** the same detections, objects, tracks and linear-fit failures as T4, to within one or
    two.
    - [0,2,3] matching: 0.76 s against T4's 25.7–27.2 s.
    - Time per frame: 5.5–5.8 s against 32.7–34.5 s.
    - So the slowdown builds up in the running process.
- **T4d, heap probe** (`tests/t4d_heap_probe.py`, one thread, idle priority during T5):
  - **Why:** pyOpenLPT's `Matrix` keeps its elements in `new T[n]`, so every `Pt2D`/`Pt3D` is a heap block, and each
    tracked point keeps about 11 heap blocks alive.
  - **How:** time `project()` and `lineOfSight()` in a fresh process, then with millions of `Pt3D` held and half of them
    freed.
  - **Result:** 1.5–1.9× slower; normal again once everything is freed.
- **T6c, the removed tracks** (`tests/t6c_deleted_tracks.py`, both runs).
  - **Why:** your questions of 5 Oct: which parts take the time, whether to change the threshold or check
    acceleration, and whether the deleted tracks are unreliable.
  - **What it measures:**
    - the size of the jump that ends a long track, against constant-velocity and constant-acceleration predictions;
    - which other rules would have kept these tracks;
    - whether a new track picks the particle up again;
    - how many cameras see where tracks end, and where in the volume it happens;
    - how far the short tracks move.
- **Run length changed (your choice, 4 Oct: "Stop T4 at ~510"):**
  - T4 had slowed to 32 s per frame, so I stopped it after frame 504. Its tracks are saved at frame 500.
  - T5 runs frames 0–500 (`config.json` `frames.t5`).
  - T4 and T5 are compared over frames 0–500.

## 6. Choices to confirm

1. **The view volume:** the old box mapped into the new frame, i.e. the same physical region as the old run, clipped to
   the water. The alternative is the whole region the cameras see in the channel.
2. **The voxel size:** 0.105 × s mm, so that every voxel-based setting keeps its physical size. The alternative is
   keeping 0.105 mm, which shrinks the search radii by 1/s.
3. **Frames 0–1000 from scratch, for both camera sets.** The old run's slow part (frames 3500–4700) enters only
   through T3.
4. **26 threads, as before.**
5. **The folder on X: is named `stb_new_calib`.**
