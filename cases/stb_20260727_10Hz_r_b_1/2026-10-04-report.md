## 1. Summary

**Speed.** The same build, config and machine, frames 0–500 from scratch:
- **Totals:** the new camFiles need 2.18 h of frame time against 2.99 h, 27 % less. With image loading and saves the
  wall time is 2.56 h against 3.28 h.
- **Per frame:** 6.1 s against 7.1 s at the start, and 24.4 s against 35.9 s over frames 300–499.

**Both runs slow down 4–5× within 500 frames, and this slowdown is the main cost.**
- **It builds up inside the running process,** not in the calibration or the images.
  - **On raw images** a frame costs the same at frame 4,600 as at frame 0 (T3).
  - **After a restart:** the same frames, with the same tracks, run 6× faster in a fresh process (5.6 s against
    33.7 s), with identical results (T4c).
- **Likely cause: memory allocation.** Every `Pt2D`/`Pt3D` in pyOpenLPT is a separate heap block. Stereo matching
  creates and frees millions of them per frame from 26 threads, while the tracks keep millions of the same sizes
  alive (T4d).
- **Update (5 Oct, night), from `tests/stb_improve/2026-10-05-report.md`:** memory allocation was the cause, but
  mostly not the `Pt2D`/`Pt3D` blocks: keeping those inside the object (A1) made every frame 1.65× faster and kept the
  slowdown. The growth was in one IPR round (cameras 0, 2, 3), whose search copied vectors at every step and
  allocated a 19–43 KB `seen` array for every candidate enumeration; with that search rewritten without allocations
  (B3) the time per frame stays flat (2.5 s over 300 frames, against HEAD's 6.4 → 16.9 s). The T4c "identical
  results" held for the first frames after the restart only; later the restarted run drifts very slightly (positions
  are saved to 1e-6 mm).
- **This made the old runs so slow:** 110 s per frame by frame 4,600 in `log.txt`.

**Tracks removed in the convergence phase.**
- **The counts:** the new camFiles remove about 1,340 long tracks per frame (10 % of 13,350 active); the old ones about
  640 (8 % of 7,910).
- **96 % of the removed are spurious.** They are tracks of 4–6 frames that fail the linear-fit test right after they
  are promoted.
  - 70–85 % of the short tracks jump more than 0.5 mm per frame, while real particles move about 0.1 mm per frame.
  - So these are chains of ghost objects, made possible by the short-track linking radius of 3.9–5.7 mm (30 voxels).
  - Removing them is right.
- **Real long tracks end at about 0.37 % per frame** (0.22 % with the old camFiles).
  - Each ends with a 3 px jump, inside the region all four cameras see, and the particle is not tracked again.
  - Removing these is also right; only the bad last point stays in the saved track.
- **The net result favours the new camFiles:**
  - 69 % more active long tracks;
  - 77 % more tracked points in long tracks (5.56 against 3.14 million over 500 frames);
  - 73 % more tracks of 200 frames or longer.

**Matching.**
- **The old camFiles never match a particle on all four cameras at 2 px.**
  - The cause is the straight epipolar line of stereo matching (`makeLine2DFromLOS3D`), drawn as a tangent at a wall
    that the old calibration places 110–190 mm from the volume.
  - Even with perfect data, 0 % of the particles seen by all four cameras could be matched at 2 or 3 px (T2).
- **With the new camFiles,** 37 % of them can be matched at 2 px with perfect data, and 91 % would be if the line
  were anchored inside the volume.

**Precision.**
- **The new camFiles:** tracked particles have a position noise of 0.005–0.006 mm per axis, about 0.1 px.
- **The old camFiles:** the same to within 20 % (0.005–0.007 mm in true scale).
  - Shaking refines each tracked particle locally.
  - The old calibration's errors show up as failed 4-camera matches and systematic offsets, not as jitter along a
    track.

**Your three questions (5 Oct):**
1. **What takes the time:** see section 3.5.
   - **In an aged process,** stereo matching, mostly the [0,2,3] combination.
   - **In a fresh one:** matching 3.5 s, IPR shaking 1.4 s, everything else 1.2 s, loading images 0.9 s.
   - **Saves** take 9–15 min every 500 frames.
2. **Change the threshold, or check acceleration?** Neither (section 3.7).
   - The point that ends a long track is a 3 px jump; the linear-fit residual shows only 0.3× of it.
   - A velocity-plus-acceleration predictor misses by the same 0.20 mm.
   - A looser limit would accept the outlier and let more spurious short tracks through.
3. **Are the deleted tracks unreliable?** The short ones are; the long ones are reliable up to the point before the

## 3. Results

### 3.1 T0: the two calibrations' frames

The new camFiles describe the rig in another world frame:
- **Origin and axes:** the origin is at the wand's mean position; Z is the C1/C2 wall's normal and Y points up.
- **The rotation:** the old frame is the new one turned 177.4° about Z, a half-turn about the C1/C2 wall's normal. So
  x and y change sign, and the old y pointed down.
- **The scale:** lengths are s = 1.229 times the old frame's.

**How the mapping was found:**
- A 21³ grid filling the old view volume was projected with the old cameras, and those pixels were triangulated with
  the new cameras.
- The similarity fit leaves 0.78 mm RMS (2.5 mm max). An affine fit is barely better (0.71 mm), so the old frame is a
  similar copy of the new one to about 1 mm.
- In the new cameras the old pixels miss by 2.8–4.3 px median and up to 40 px at the box's corners: the two
  calibrations disagree by that much.

**The new config:**
- **View volume:** x [−81, 54], y [−42, 59], z [−36, 52.5] mm. This is the old box mapped into the new frame and
  clipped 1 mm inside the channel's far wall, 100 mm from the C1/C2 wall.
- **Voxel:** 0.129 mm (0.105 × s), so every voxel-based setting keeps its physical size.
- **Coverage:** four cameras see 64 % of the new box and three or more see 77 %. In the old calibration's own view the
  old box had 73 % and 85 %.

**The cameras:** mapped into the new frame, the old calibration puts the cameras 290–380 mm closer than the new one.
That fits its focal length of 13,333 px against 18,800–22,000 px.

### 3.2 T1: the projection tolerance costs nothing

- **Time:** with the delivered 1e-12 / 1000 iterations, `project()` takes 1.4–1.5 µs per call in the view volume and
  1.3–1.5 µs on the points that matching projects. With 1e-6 / 50 it is the same to within noise.
- **Results:** no failures on 100,000 + 36,000 points per camera; the two settings differ by at most 3e-5 px.
- **The old camFiles** cost the same. So T1's rule for a T4 variant did not trigger.

### 3.3 T2: the straight epipolar lines of stereo matching

How stereo matching finds candidates (`StereoMatch::makeLine2DFromLOS3D`):
- It projects two points 1 and 2 mm past a camera's far wall along each line of sight, and draws the straight 2-D line
  through them in the other cameras.
- Through a flat window the true image of the line of sight is a curve.
- This line is its tangent at the wall, extrapolated across the whole volume.
- A match must lie within the 2-D tolerance of every such line, in every pair of its cameras: forward lines and
  back-projections.

For 20,000 random points of each view volume, with exact pixels (no calibration or detection error), the share of
the points seen by every camera of a combination that matching can still find:

| combination | new camFiles, 2 px | 3 px | old camFiles, 2 px | 3 px | line anchored inside the volume (new), 2 px |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [0,1,2,3] | 37 % | 54 % | 0 % | 0 % | 91 % |
| [0,1,2] | 44 % | 61 % | 0 % | 0 % | 97 % |
| [0,1,3] | 43 % | 60 % | 17 % | 36 % | 91 % |
| [0,2,3] | 42 % | 59 % | 0 % | 0 % | 97 % |
| [1,2,3] | 41 % | 57 % | 0 % | 0 % | 92 % |

**How far the lines miss:**
- Between cameras behind the same wall the error stays under 1 px.
- Between cameras behind different walls it grows with the distance from the source camera's wall: 0.5 px median
  (2.4 px p95) at 50–60 mm, and 1.4 px median (7.2 px p95) at 90–100 mm.

**Why the old camFiles do worse:**
- The old calibration puts its walls 110–190 mm from its view volume, against 3–10 mm for the new one.
- So its tangents are extrapolated much farther, and its pairs across the walls miss by 9 px median.

**This explains the old logs:**
- Four-camera matching found 0 particles at 2 px in every frame.
- [0,1,3] found the most of the 3-camera sets, and [0,2,3] and [1,2,3] almost none at 2 px.

### 3.6 T4c and T4d: why the tracking slows down

**T4c, the restart test.**
- A fresh process loaded T4's active tracks at frame 500 (13,642 long, 8,923 short) and ran frames 501–600. It used
  the same camFiles, config and images as T4.
- Frames 501–505 produced the same detections, objects, tracks and linear-fit failures as T4, to within one or two.

| frames 501–505, medians | T4 (process 500 frames old) | T4c (fresh process) |
|---|---|---|
| time per frame | 33.7 s | 5.6 s |
| matching [0,2,3] | 26.3 s | 0.77 s |
| matching [0,1,2] | 1.86 s | 0.60 s |
| matching [0,1,2,3] | 1.62 s | 1.01 s |
| objects found | 10,459 | 10,461 |
| active long tracks | 13,615 | 13,615 |

**The slowdown starts again in a fresh process, sooner when it holds more tracks:**
- **T4c:** [0,2,3] goes from 0.8 s to 4.8 s after 100 frames, and the frame from 5.6 s to 9.8 s.
  - It started with 13,600 long tracks and began slowing after about 35 frames.
- **T4:** it started with almost none and began slowing after about 85 frames.
- **T5 (old camFiles)** slows down even more: 7.1 s at frame 100, 11.6 s at 200, 26.4 s at 300, 36.1 s at 400 and
  47.6 s at 499.

**Likely cause (strongly supported, not proven): the heap.**
- **Every geometry value is its own heap block.** pyOpenLPT's `Matrix` keeps its elements in `new T[n]`, so every
  `Pt2D`, `Pt3D` and `Line3D` is a separate heap block.
  - Projection and line of sight create and free many such blocks per call.
  - Matching calls them millions of times per frame, from 26 threads.
- **Tracks keep blocks of the same sizes alive.** Every tracked point keeps about 11 heap blocks for its track's
  whole life: the object, its `Pt3D`, a vector, four 2-D objects and their `Pt2D`.
  - That is about 37 million blocks by frame 500 in T4 (3.4 million points in active tracks).
- **What the measurements show:**
  - Both runs used fewer and fewer cores as they slowed: medians of 8.1 and 6.3 over the run, from about 19–23 at the
    start. That looks like threads waiting on the heap.
  - A fresh process reloads the same tracks compactly and runs fast.
  - The slowdown grows faster with more tracks held.
  - [0,2,3], the combination with the most projections (camera 0's 40,000 detections), suffers most.
- **T4d** shows the mechanism on one thread. Holding 12 million `Pt3D` blocks and freeing every other one makes
  `project()` 1.5× and `lineOfSight()` 1.7× slower (1.6× and 1.9× after more churn of the same size). Freeing them all
  restores the speed. With 26 threads competing for the heap, the effect is larger.

The old run's growth from 6 s to 110 s per frame (`log.txt`) is the same effect:
- It was fast right after it loaded tracks at frame 3500.
- It slowed down over about 500 frames, with constant detections, objects and tracks.
- [0,2,3] grew 33×, [0,1,2] 8× and [1,2,3] 1.5×.

### 3.7 T6b and T6c: the tracks that are kept and the tracks that are removed

**The tracked particles** (T6b; the old run's lengths are multiplied by s = 1.229 to true scale):

| | new camFiles | old camFiles |
|---|---|---|
| speed, median per track (p10–p90) | 0.099 mm/frame (0.082–0.121) | 0.094 mm/frame (0.081–0.119) |
| position noise per axis, median over tracks | 0.005 / 0.006 / 0.005 mm (x / y / z) | 0.005 / 0.007 / 0.006 mm |
| linear-fit residual of the points that passed, median / p99 | 0.005 / 0.038 mm | 0.007 / 0.046 mm |
| residual of the point that ended a lost track, median (p10–p90) | 0.057 mm (0.051–0.072) | 0.068 mm (0.062–0.082) |

**The linear-fit test hides the size of the jump** (T6c, the new camFiles; the old ones are the same within 10 %):
- 23,605 of the 24,273 lost long tracks (≥ 9 points) ended by the linear-fit test.
- Their last point lies 0.19 mm, about 3 px, from where the 6 points before it predict. That is a median of 32× the
  track's own noise.
- The test fits its line through the last point too, which pulls the line toward it. So its residual shows only about
  0.3× of the jump: 0.057 mm.

**It is not curvature:**
- A constant-acceleration predictor misses by the same 0.20 mm.
- The fitted acceleration over the 7 points before the end is 0.008 mm/frame² (median; p90 0.024).
- A 0.05 mm residual would need 0.1 mm/frame², about 2,500 m/s², so at 5 kHz acceleration plays no part.

**Other rules at that point:**
- A limit of 0.1 mm, or a 5-point quadratic fit at 0.05 mm, would have kept all of these tracks. It would have done so
  by accepting a 3 px outlier.
- A noise-relative limit of 6–10× the track's own sigma would have kept 8–50 %.

**The particles do not come back:**
- Another track starts near where the lost particle should be within 10 frames for only 0.2 % of the lost tracks
  (0.2 mm, matching velocity).
- Even within 1 mm and with any velocity, it is only 2.2 %. Within 30 frames at 0.5 mm it is 1.2 %.

**They are lost inside the measured volume:**
- 99 % of the last good points are seen by all four cameras.
- The tracks that leave the view volume end where only cameras 0 and 1 see them (96 %), as they should.

**Where it happens:**
- With the new camFiles, losses are 2–3× more frequent per tracked point in the corner farthest from both windows
  (high x and z). At the lowest y most bins have only 0.1–0.4 of the mean.
- With the old camFiles they are spread more evenly (p10–p90 0.79–1.27 against 0.68–2.04).
- Something local makes particles vanish there. Two candidates:
  - bubbles masking particles: the images are bubble-masked;
  - dimmer images far from the cameras.

**The short tracks:**
- Of the 2- and 3-point tracks active at frame 500, the median displacement is 1.3–1.4 mm per frame.
- 70–85 % move more than 0.5 mm per frame, and only 9–23 % move like the tracked particles (0.06–0.16 mm per frame).
- The old camFiles give the same shares.
- They come from the convergence phase's short-track linking. It takes the nearest new object within 30 voxels of the
  predicted position (3.9 mm, growing to 5.7 mm), and IPR offers 5,000–11,000 new objects per frame.
- So most links are chance links, and the linear-fit test removes them once they reach four points: about 1,270 per
  frame with the new camFiles.

## 4. What I propose next (each needs your go-ahead)

**Your decisions (5 Oct):**
- **1 and 2:** go ahead, with a save-frequency input and a JSON input file added to 1.
- **3:** keep it for now, marked as deferred.
- **4:** after 1 and 2.
- You also asked about the track prediction and a Kalman filter.
- The plan for all of it is `tests/stb_improve/2026-10-05-plan.md` (waiting for approval). The list below is the
  original proposal.

1. **Stop the slowdown: the largest gain.** With it gone, frames would stay at about 7 s; they now reach 30–50 s by
   frame 500 and 110 s later.
   - **Now, without a code change:** run in chunks of about 100–200 frames, each restarted from the previous chunk's
     saved tracks (`config.txt`: load flag and frame). T4c shows a restart gives the same results.
     - Write `Results` to a local disk: saving to X: took 9–15 min per 500 frames.
   - **Code:** build pyopenlpt with a scalable allocator (for example mimalloc) and rerun T4 for 200 frames. If the
     slowdown disappears, the cause is confirmed.
     - The lasting fix is a `Pt2D`/`Pt3D` that does not allocate on the heap. It also makes everything else faster.
2. **Matching itself (3.5 s per frame in a fresh process):**
   - cache each detection's line of sight and its 2-D lines in the other cameras, once per IPR round; today they are
     recomputed for every candidate and every back-projection check;
   - anchor the epipolar line inside the view volume. T2: 37 % of the 4-camera particles can be found at 2 px today,
     91 % then.
   - Both are code changes in `StereoMatch.cpp`. Check them by T2, T3 and a short T4.
3. **Fewer spurious short tracks (config only):** lower "Avg interparticle spacing" in `tracerConfig.txt` from 30 to
   about 5–8 voxels (0.65–1.0 mm), and test it on 200 frames.
   - The particles move 0.08–0.15 mm per frame, so their links stay inside that radius.
   - Expected: most of the about 1,270 removals per frame go away, along with their linking and shaking work.
4. **The linear-fit test:** keep the 0.05 mm limit, but improve two things (code changes in `STB.cpp`):
   - drop the failing point before a track is saved as inactive (today each lost track ends with its 3 px outlier);
   - make the limit a config setting in mm, ideally tested against a prediction from the earlier points.
   - Raising the limit, or adding acceleration, would keep outliers and spurious tracks rather than more real
     particles.
5. **Loading images:** 0.9 s per frame from X:. A local copy, or loading the next frame while the current one is
   tracked, saves about 12 %.
6. **The corner where particles are lost:** compare the losses with your bubble tracks (`Results_bb`, mapped into the
   new frame by T0's similarity). That tells whether bubbles mask the particles.
