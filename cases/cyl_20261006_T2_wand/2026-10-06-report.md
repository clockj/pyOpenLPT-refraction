## 4. The cameras' move

- **Seen first in P0:** T0's calibration on the surveyed frames left cam1's residuals offset by +0.49 px and cam2's
  by −0.54 px, both vertical. On T0's own frames both are 0.00.
- **X1 on all frames:**
  - **The step:** T0's cameras on T2 disagree by +1.010 px (cam1 minus cam2), and T2's cameras on T0 by −1.046 px.
  - **The other run:** T0's cameras on T0 and T2's on T2 sit at −0.001 and 0.000 px.
  - **The wand lengths:** the change is too small for them to notice (+1.7 and −5.0 µm mean).
  - **The figure:** `tests/x1_offsets.png`, and the page's chart.
- **Why only vertical:** the two cameras stand side by side around the vertical tube, so their lines of sight to a
  ball can miss each other only up or down. A sideways move would be absorbed into the balls' depth, and this test
  cannot see it.
- **What it means:** something changed by about 1 px between 14:25 and 15:28: a camera tilted by 26 µrad, a
  shift of 26 µm at the wand, or a lens's focus. A calibration holds only while nothing in the cameras is touched.
  Record the wand right before or after each experiment.
- **The open question:** whether anything was done at the rig between the recordings. If nothing was, the mounts
  settle on their own.

## 6. What this means

1. **Use T2's camFiles with T2, and T0's with T0**, each only inside the volume its wand covered. Neither fixes f or
   the distances.
2. **Calibrate close in time to each experiment, without touching the rig in between.** The cameras moved by about
   1 px within an hour.
3. **Fix f independently.**
   - **Why it would work:** every wand fit of both runs lies on one line per camera, and the dot board's cam1 lies on
     that line too. A dot-board f for each camera, held in the wand calibration, would pin the rest.
   - **What is missing:** `cylinder_wand` cannot hold f yet. It is a small library option that needs your approval.
4. **A thinner rod (≤ 1 mm) or larger balls**, and more depth: as for T0. The errors run along the rod (section 1).
