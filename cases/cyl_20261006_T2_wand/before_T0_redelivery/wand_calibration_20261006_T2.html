The recording

1,966 frames show the whole wand in both cameras

The wand swings through the same small views as in T0 (about 26 × 21 mm and 29 × 23 mm at the wand), more often end-on to a camera. About 25 s in, the light got brighter and stayed so; the detector divides by the background, so it found the balls alike before and after.

Finding the balls

T0's detector settings, unchanged

The same wand at the same magnification: the balls' image sizes are within 1 % of T0's, and the rod opening of 95 px still separates them. The rod mask of 50 px has the limit found in T0: the 2 mm rod covers about 75 % of the small ball's height, so its centre depends on the mask by 0.6–2 px (see the T0 page).

The calibration

Two cameras 61° apart, about 1 m from the tube

Seen along the tube's axis. The fit puts cam1 1,001 mm and cam2 1,085 mm from the axis (T0: 1,010 and 1,042 mm). As in T0, the recording cannot pin these distances down (below), so their difference from T0's says nothing about the cameras.

Mean residual in each image cell with 20 or more detections, the arrow drawn 500× (0.1 px = 50 px): up to 0.76 px in cam1 and 0.84 px in cam2. In cam1 they point mostly across the image, outward on both sides. Freeing the lens distortion k1 does not remove them (T5), so they are not lens distortion; as in T0, the likely source is the rod's effect on the ball centres, which follows the wand's pose.

New in T2

The cameras moved by about 1.0 px between the two recordings

Two cameras side by side around the tube must agree on the height of every ball. T0's calibration still measures T2's wand at the right length, but its two cameras now disagree about the balls' height by 1.01 px, and T2's calibration disagrees on T0's frames by the same amount the other way (−1.05 px). One px is a tilt of 26 µrad, a shift of 26 µm at the wand, or a lens refocused. This test cannot say which camera changed, nor see a sideways move.

The focal length

T2 does not fix f either: its parts spread along the same line as T0's

Long lenses looking at a 30 mm volume from 1 m see almost no perspective, so a camera farther away with a longer lens makes nearly the same image. T2's wand covered about T0's volume, with no more depth, so it cannot separate the two any better.

What still holds

Inside the wand's volume T2's cameras agree

Each part's cameras triangulate every kept frame of T2, including the frames they were not fitted on. Despite their different f, the wand lengths they measure on the held-out frames agree with 10 mm to within 0.06 % on average over all six parts (32–44 µm RMS). In T0 the halves gave 0.07–0.13 %.

What this means

Using these calibrations, and the next recording

  1. Use T2's camFiles for T2, and T0's for T0, each only inside the volume its wand covered. Neither fixes f or the distances, so do not rely on them outside that volume.
  2. Record the wand right before (or after) each experiment, without touching the rig in between. The cameras moved by about 1 px between T0 and T2.
  3. Check what changed between T0 and T2: a camera, its mount or lens touched, or the light moved. If nothing was touched, the mounts settle on their own and should be checked.
  4. The focal length needs an independent measurement: the dot board through the tube for each camera, held fixed in the wand calibration (a small library option, not built yet), or a third camera.
  5. A thinner rod (1 mm or less) or larger balls, and more depth: as for T0.
