When the flat-window calibration cannot work

What to watch in a real experiment. From every test of plate_wand.calibrate: when its answer can be wrong, when it is right but imprecise, what does not matter, and what was never tested. For each, the evidence, how calibration_report.txt shows it, and what to do.

Terms. Each answer is compared with the fit started from the truth on the same data. The same minimum: every parameter within 0.01σ of it, the same detections kept. Equivalent: every parameter within 3σ and the RMS within 2 %, or a lower RMS. Right means at least equivalent, wrong neither. All data are synthetic: detections from pyOpenLPT's C++ camera model with Gaussian noise. No real wand data have been calibrated yet.

In short

Seven things to watch, most important first

  1. Wrong answers come from one rig feature: a window seen only by one or two cameras with narrow views. Narrow means each sees the wand under less than about 6°: a long lens, or a camera far from the wand's volume. The calibration now scans such a window's normal itself, which fixed 2 of the 5 known wrong answers. The worst rig tested has two cameras, each alone behind its own window with a long lens (seed 1045).
  2. Window-normal hints help, and never made an answer worse. Within 20° of the truth they gave a right answer in 45 of 50 runs on the five hardest cases, every miss on seed 1045. Over W5 and R4 with hints 0–10° off, none of 120 cases was worse, and the three still wrong without hints were right.
  3. Detection noise near 1 px on a rig with several windows lets the fit stop pinned against a window (6 of the 8 pinned answers came at 1 px). The restarts fix most of them. Aim for about 0.1 px.
  4. The wand length is the only scale. Measure it to 0.1 % or better: the data cannot check it.
  5. An unmodelled lens distortion hides in the residuals. If the lens may distort, fit k1.
  6. Calibrate over the whole measurement volume. The error grows outside it, 2× at twice the volume.
  7. Read the WARNINGS block of calibration_report.txt first. At 0.1 px every wrong answer in the tests had a warning.

When the answer can be wrong

Every wrong answer had a weakly seen window

A window's tilt changes a narrow-view camera's images only slightly, so the cost has several minima. The truth's valley is a few degrees wide, and a wrong answer 10–50° away is a separate minimum behind a ridge. Restarts that perturb the answer by its own error bars do not leave it (diagnosis D8). Since 3 Oct the calibration scans the normal of every such window, and keeps what it finds only when it fits the data better.

Window-normal hints

A rough hint is enough, except with one long-lens camera per window

A hint is a rough guess of each window's normal, as each camera sees it. The calibration builds its start three ways (without the hints, with each hinted normal held, and with a second search about each hint), refits each with its own restarts, and keeps a hinted answer only when it fits the data better. The final fit estimates every normal from the data: the hint only steers the start.

When to give them
For a window seen only by one or two narrow-view cameras. A first calibration's WARNINGS block names such windows.
What to give
Per camera, the normal of its window in the camera's own frame: x along the image rows, y down the image, z along the optical axis, either sign. A camera turned 30° to the left of square-on, about its vertical axis, sees (sin 30°, 0, cos 30°). In the rig file, null for a camera without a hint: 
"hints": {"normals": [[0.5, 0, 0.866], null, [0, -0.17, 0.98], null]}
How accurate
Windows with two cameras: up to 20° was always enough (3110, 1031); at 30°, 3 of 3 runs on 3110 and 2 of 3 on 1031. A two-camera window with two single long-lens windows (1034): up to 30°, all 13 runs right. One camera per window with long lenses (1045): right with an exact hint and in 2 of 3 runs at 5°, mostly wrong beyond.
Never worse
None of the 65 hinted runs on the hard cases, and none of the 120 W5 and R4 cases hinted 0–10° off, was worse than without hints. The three cases still wrong without hints were right with them; their hints happened to be within 1–10°, 1045's within 1°.
What the report says
Each hint's angle from the answer. A large angle means a rough hint or a wrong answer.
Cost
With hints a calibration takes a median 2.55× as long (1.2–3.4×).

Tested, and it does not matter

  • The window constants: a plate 10 % too thick, or a water index 0.002 off, left the 3-D shape error within 1 µm (W8).
  • The angle between windows: 30°–180° all reach the global minimum (A1: 56 of 56).
  • A camera looking straight through its window: no loss, also next to angled cameras.
  • Steep views, up to 45° incidence (W2 rig D).
  • Bad detections: 2 % gross outliers, 1 % swapped ends, a camera missing from 40 % of the frames, one camera five times noisier (W7).
  • No frame seen by every camera (W7).

Never tested

  • Real data (W10).
  • Lens distortion fitted from scratch: k1 was fitted only from the truth (W8).
  • Windows of two or more plates in a calibration. The model handles them (W0).
  • Other window angles with a single camera or long lenses: A1 used two moderate-lens cameras per window.
  • More than 3 windows or 6 cameras; cameras without a window.
  • Ball-centre bias through a window: elliptical images, as the tube's T13 found.
  • Image-fixed patterns, noise varying with depth, a bending wand, unsynchronised cameras.

The report's warnings

What calibration_report.txt flags

The report opens with a WARNINGS block; calibration_result.json has the same list under "warnings", each with a code. Each warning gives its evidence and what to do. Below, how often each fired in H5a: the 120 W5 and R4 cases rerun without hints on the final code (3 wrong, 117 right).

At 0.1 px every wrong answer had a warning (3 of 3) and only 3 of the 47 right answers did. At 0.5–1 px almost every multi-window case gets noisy, as it should. No warning does not prove the answer right: it means none of the conditions the tests linked to wrong or weak answers is present.

Checklist

For the experiment, in order

1Design the rig

  1. Every window: two or more cameras, one with a wide enough view. The wand's positions should span about f/9 pixels of that camera's image diagonal or more, a view of 6°. With f = 20,000 px that is about 2,100 px; a 100 mm cloud seen from 1 m spans 5.7°, just too narrow.
  2. Never one camera per window with long lenses (seed 1045's rig).
  3. If a window must have only narrow-view cameras, plan window-normal hints for them: within about 5° for a window with a single camera.
  4. Lines of sight 20° or more apart, 35–40° better. Perpendicular windows give the best shape.
  5. Synchronise the cameras, or move the wand slowly.

2Before recording

  1. Measure the wand length to 0.1 % or better (25 µm on 25 mm).
  2. Enter the windows' plate thicknesses and indices from the camera side. They are not critical.
  3. Make the two ends distinguishable, for example with two ball sizes.
  4. If the lens may distort, plan to fit k1 (fit_k1=True).

3While recording

  1. About 1,000 frames with both ends seen by two or more cameras.
  2. The wand through the whole volume, its full depth included.
  3. Sharp, well-lit detections: about 0.1 px.
  4. Frames shared between the cameras of different windows: at least 3 per pair of window groups, ideally many.

4After calibrating

  1. Read the WARNINGS block first. weak_window or noisy on a rig with several windows is the main sign of a possibly wrong answer.
  2. Read each window's and camera's σ. Degrees on a normal, or tens of mm on a distance, mean the data hold it loosely.
  3. Compare the fit's noise with a still wand's. It should not be clearly larger.
  4. Validate on something of known size: a second wand, or a dot target triangulated through the window.
  5. Plot each camera's residuals over the image. A pattern fixed to the image hides in the RMS.

What changed on 3 Oct

Fixed as far as the tests reach

All in plate_wand.py and plate_wand_io.py. Without hints, a rig with no weak window gives exactly the answer it gave before: in H5a, 118 of the 120 W5 and R4 answers are unchanged, and the other 2 are better.

  • Hints run three start paths, each with its own restarts (H3). A hinted answer replaces the default one only when its capped cost is lower and it is another minimum, or keeps the same observations.
  • A weak window's normal is scanned (H4): a 30°-wide grid of fits with that normal held, 3° apart, then the best minima released and kept on the same terms. Without hints it fixes R4 3110 and W5 1001, and adds about 2–13 min to a rig with weak windows (32 calibrations at once; less on a machine running one).
  • The report warns (H5): seven warnings, each with its evidence and what to do.
  • Tests: H3a, D8, H4a, H4b, H5a and H1b, and pytest: 12 fast checks and 4 slow, all passing. Nothing is committed.
