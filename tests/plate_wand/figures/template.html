What the flat-window wand-calibration tests show

Every number below comes from synthetic data. Wand detections were generated by pyOpenLPT's C++ flat-window camera (PINPLATE): air, a 12.7 mm acrylic wall (n 1.49), water (n 1.333), with 0.1 px of noise per coordinate unless stated. The calibration receives only the detections, the wand length, the image sizes, each window's thickness and indices, and which camera looks through which window. It gets no camera positions, no focal lengths and no window positions or orientations.

01The tests and the rigs

    
Twelve checks, from the camera model to calibrations from scratch, and a thirteenth on real data still to come. Most use five reference rigs, A to E, each built around a 100 mm cube of wand positions centred 150 mm behind a 12.7 mm acrylic wall (rig E: a 16 mm cube, 100 mm behind). W5 adds 60 random rigs, and W6 runs the GUI's current calibration on the same data.

    

    
  


  

    
02Calibration from scratch (W5)

    
Sixty random rigs: 2–6 cameras, 1–3 walls of a box tank, acrylic 6–25.4 mm thick, incidence 0–45°, 150–1,500 mm from the wall, focal lengths 1,500–40,000 px, wand clouds 30–300 mm. Fifty have 0.1 px of noise and ten have 1 px. Each rig was calibrated twice: from nothing, and once started at the true cameras and windows, which finds the best answer the data allow. A run from nothing passes when it reaches that same answer: every parameter within 0.01 σ, the same detections kept, and the same RMS.

    
    
  


  

    
03Against the GUI's calibration (W6)

    
The GUI's current refractive calibration and the new one were run on the same data: rigs A–D at 0.1 and 0.5 px, and the first ten W5 rigs at 0.1 px, 1,000 frames each, every frame seen by every camera. The GUI was given the true focal lengths in its camera table; the new calibration got none. Each answer is judged by its exported camera files, loaded through the C++: 500 unseen points in the cloud, with exact pixels, are triangulated with it and fitted to the truth by the best rigid motion.

    
  


  

    
04Precision and error bars (W2, W3)

    
First what the data can determine, computed from the information matrix at the truth without fitting (the Cramér–Rao bound, 0.1 px, 1,000 frames). Then whether real fits reach it: 40 noisy data sets per rig, each fitted from the truth, with every error compared to the σ the bound predicts and to the σ the fit itself reports.

    
    
  


  

    
05How close the start must be (W4)

    
The final fit was started from the truth with one thing wrong, ten times per level with random directions, and passes when it reaches the fit started from the truth. This sets how coarse the start's search may be.

    
  


  

    
06Bad data (W7)

    
Rigs A and B were calibrated from scratch with the data spoiled one way at a time, then compared with the answer from the clean data.

    
  


  

    
07Wrong constants (W8)

    
The data were made with the true rig, and the calibration was given one constant wrong. The residuals cannot reveal a wrong plate thickness or water index, and neither matters much. The wand length is the scale, so its error passes straight through. Unlike through the tube, letting the wand length float is no check here: only the 12.7 mm plate carries any scale.
