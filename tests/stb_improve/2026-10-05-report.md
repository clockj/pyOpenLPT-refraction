## 1. Summary

**The slowdown is found and removed.**
- **Where:** all the growth of the time per frame was IPR's stereo matching, and almost all of that one round: the
  reduced camera set [0,2,3], the heaviest round (camera 1, left out, has by far the fewest detections). Its
  matching time grew tenfold over 300 frames (0.70 → 7.5 s at 2 px) while its detections and its search work fell.
- **What:** the same work becoming slower as the process aged, in the code that allocated most: the matching
  search. A1 (small matrices without the heap) made every frame 1.65× faster but kept the growth; the search still
  copied five vectors per step and allocated a `seen` array per enumeration, about 2 million allocations per IPR
  round. B3 (no allocations in the search) removed the growth; B1 (tables of lines of sight and image lines) alone
  did not (3.20 → 11.95 s per frame), though it made the start faster (section 4.2). The restart tests show the same:
  a fresh process started from frame 200 runs 1.9–2.2× faster than the aged one on the same frames, then slows down
  again (HEAD 5.9 → 11.5 s per frame within 100 frames).
- **Result, all changes (the final build, inline size 3):** the time per frame stays flat over 300 frames, **2.49 →
  2.48 s**, against **6.39 → 16.85 s** for HEAD: 6.8× faster at frames 281–300 and 3.9× less frame time over the 300
  frames (12.5 against 49.2 min), at HEAD's memory (peak 2.28 against 2.25 GB). A process restarted from saved tracks
  is no faster than the aged one (2.47 against 2.49 s per frame); for HEAD it was 1.9× faster.
- **Without B2** (A1, B0, B1, B3, B4) the speed is the same (2.65 → 2.58 s per frame) and the outputs are identical to
  HEAD's: every counter of every frame and all output files byte-identical, over 300 frames.
- **B2** (image lines anchored in the view volume) finds more at the same speed: IPR objects +24 %, 4-camera matches
  at 2 px +98 %, active long tracks +15 %.

**The new inputs work:** `save_interval` (A3) and `config.json` (A4), with a converter and a Python reader. Since A4b
(section 8) the GUI writes `config.json`, with a Save Interval field.

**Your questions in the plan:** the k-d tree helped where a radius query was the job (`checkRepeat`, B4: 0.56 → 0.001
s per frame); the stereo matching's grid index stays (B1 and B3 removed the cost around it).

### 4.2 Stereo matching (P3, B0's counters)

`analyze.py b` → `results/b_matching.txt`, `b_matching_time.png`; `analyze.py combo RUN` → `results/b_combo_*.txt`.
Means per frame over frames 4–300 (matching at frames 281–300 in its own column):

| run | IPR matching, s | same, frames 281–300 | `checkRepeat`, s | IPR objects | 4-camera matches at 2 px | active long tracks (281–300) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HEAD | 7.04 | 13.95 | 0.556 | 11,801 | 195 | 12,588 |
| A1 (inline 9) | 3.98 | 8.39 | 0.123 | 11,801 | 195 | 12,588 |
| A1 + B0, B1, B3, B4 | 0.77 | 0.70 | 0.001 | 11,801 | 195 | 12,588 |
| A1 + B0–B4 (B2) | 0.64 | 0.57 | 0.002 | 14,590 | 386 | 14,509 |

(The match counts are read from the lines the main thread prints; HEAD's racy detection line, section 5, cannot
drop a round from them.)

- **Where the growth was:** in HEAD the matching time of the round with cameras [0,2,3] rose from 0.70 s (2 px) and
  0.65 s (3 px) at frame 4 to 7.52 s and 5.74 s at frame 300; every other round stayed between 0.01 and 0.7 s.
  Over the same frames that round's detections fell (camera 0, the busiest, about 43,000 → 37,000) and its matches
  fell (5,076 → 3,380).
- **Work against time:** B0's counters for that round in the B build fall over the run (frames 4–23 → 95–105: 13,460
  → 12,720 reference observations, 414,000 → 352,000 search steps per round) and its time falls with them (0.30 →
  0.23 s per frame); HEAD and A1, doing the same work (identical outputs), slowed down instead (1.34 → 4.00 s and 0.74
  → 1.73 s per frame at frames 140–150).
- **Lines of sight and projections per round** (frames 0–5): 678,000 and 3.2 million before B1, 60,000 and 275,000
  with it. With B1 and B3 a round's matching takes 0.019 s for the tables and 0.061 s for the search and check
  (frames 0–50); pruning 0.002 s (2.5 %, so B5 was not done), triangulation 0.002 s.
- **Cores busy** (mean over a 300-frame run): HEAD 15.1, A1 16.1, B builds 25.6 and 25.4 of 26 threads. The HEAD
  run starts at 25 and ends at 8: the same work per frame (159 → 135 CPU-seconds) spread over fewer and fewer busy
  threads.
- **Which change removed the growth** (your question of 5 Oct: "is the key to cache the line of sight?"). A
  300-frame run of the build with B1 alone (A1 + B0 + B1: the tables, without B3 and B4), `runs/b300_b1`:

  | per frame | A1 | A1 + B1 | A1 + B1 + B3 + B4 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | frames 4–23 | 3.83 s | 3.20 s | 2.65 s |
  | frames 281–300 | 10.33 s | 11.95 s | 2.58 s |
  | growth | 2.7× | 3.7× | 1.0× |
  | round [0,2,3], frames 4–23 → 281–300 | 0.74 → 7.13 s | 0.76 → 8.61 s | 0.30 → 0.21 s |

  The tables make the start faster (matching 2.15 → 1.52 s per frame) but do not stop the growth. In the round
  [0,2,3] the B1 build and the B1+B3+B4 build do the same work (B0's counters agree: 414,000 search steps per round at
  the start, 302,000 at the end), and building the tables (0.015–0.020 s per round) and pruning (0.009–0.010 s) take
  the same time early and late in both. The difference is all in the search and check, the code B3 rewrote: with B1
  alone 0.34 s per round at the start and 4.28 s at the end (12× slower for 27 % less work), with B3 0.112 → 0.074 s.
  **So the key to removing the slowdown is the search without allocations (B3)**: no vectors copied per step, no
  `seen` array (19–43 KB, one byte per detection of the target camera) allocated and zeroed for each of about 230,000
  enumerations per round, no per-call line buffers. Caching (B1) gives the constant-factor gain and the tables B3's
  buffers read. The B1 build is identical to HEAD too (all 301 frames, 11 byte-identical files).

## 5. Findings along the way

- **HEAD is deterministic:** two runs give identical counters and byte-identical outputs.
- **HEAD's IPR log line is racy:** the detections per camera were printed from inside a parallel loop, in the
  threads' finishing order, and sometimes with interleaved digits (`5042359`); a parser then misses that round.
  Fixed (log only). The comparisons read the rounds from lines the main thread prints.
- **The repo's C++ tests are stale:** 13 of 16 do not compile at HEAD, and the non-Python CMake configuration failed
  (fixed in `cmake/openLPT.cmake`).
- **AddressSanitizer is ~80× slower per frame on HEAD** (every one of the millions of allocations per frame is
  tracked); the runs cover frames 0–5.
- **The case's T4c restart test** (a fresh process 6× faster than the aged one on the same frames) is explained: the
  aged process ran the same matching work more slowly.

### 10.1 Your three questions, answered

1. **Can the track prediction be improved?** Not in a way that changes the tracking.

### 10.2 What today's predictor does

Read again for this phase (`STB::predictNext`), and checked with SymPy (`e1_sympy_check.py`, 35 checks: 28 proved,
6 numbers recomputed, 1 skipped):
- **The formula:** its LMS loop stops after one pass, because one normalised step solves its single equation.
  So, per axis, from the last m + 1 positions (m = min(n − 1, 5)): p = y_m · Σ y_j y_(j+1) / Σ y_j² (j < m),
  with 10 mm added to the axis first when |y_m| < 1 mm.
- **Far from the origin** this is the mean step: p = y_m + (y_m − y_0)/m (MD below).
- **Near the origin** it is pushed away from 0.
  - For linear motion, with a coordinate a and a step b, the error is (m+1) b² / (2a) to first order.
  - For m = 5 it is exactly b² (3a + 4b) / ((a + 2b)² + 2b²), which points away from 0 whenever the six positions
    lie on one side of it.
  - At 0.1 mm/frame that is 0.009–0.037 mm at 1–3 mm from 0, and 0.003 mm at 10 mm. Every axis of the view volume
    crosses 0.
- **Under acceleration A** the mean step misses by 3A; a 6-point least-squares line by 14A/3.
