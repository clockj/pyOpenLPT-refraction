## 1. The answers

You asked:

> will vsc (using reliable long tracks to further optimize camera parameters) help?

**Yes, modestly, with two passes and a tighter tolerance.** T1 tracked at 1.0 px on frames corrected twice gives 11 %
more real long tracks than today's run by the counts estimate (329 against 296 at frame 100) with a fifth fewer
ghosts (39 against 50 %). One pass alone gives purer tracks, not more. The gain is bounded because the calibration is
a small part of what limits T1; the shape model and the detections are the larger part (the study's finding).
- **T1's tracks show a real calibration pattern.** The offset of the tracked pairs across the epipolar curves has a
  regional pattern of 0.32 px RMS, in cam2's v. It is the same in both halves of the recording (correlation 0.80) and
  the same without tracks (all candidate pairs, correlation 0.97). It doesn't depend on depth once an image field
  takes it up (V0).
- **A field fitted on half the frames sharpens the other half's true pairs.** The share within 1 px of the curve goes
  from 65 to 72 %. At 1.0 px the matcher keeps 12 % more true pairs, at 0.75 px 21 % more, with the random pairs
  unchanged (V1).
- **End to end on T1** (frames warped by the field, no library change; real long tracks at frame 100 by the counts
  estimate, which follows the true gain on synthetic cases):
  - at today's 1.5 px one pass changes nothing (296 → 286);
  - at 1.0 px one pass gives a purer set (ghosts 50 → 44 %) with as many real tracks (290);
  - at 1.0 px **two passes give 329 real tracks (+11 %) with 39 % ghosts**; the swirl estimate, which understates
    gains, gives 279 against 278 and ghosts 48 against 53 %;
  - tightening to 1.0 px without the correction loses real tracks (268): the correction is what makes the tighter
    tolerance pay (+23 % real tracks at 1.0 px after two passes);
  - the tracked pairs lose their systematic offset (median +0.08 → −0.02 px) and tighten (robust SD 0.95 → 0.74 px).
- **Why not more:** on the case matched to T1 (Gallm), the calibration error costs only a small part of what is lost
  (recall 0.150 → 0.131; the clean case's is 0.705); the shape model's error and the particles throughout the tube cost
  far more, as the study found. A correction can at most recover that part.

**With more cameras (you asked for the synthetic test with different numbers of cameras):**
- **The same 0.7 px error field costs 2 cameras the most** (recall 0.705 → 0.58, ghosts 11 → 22 %), 3 cameras less
  (0.877 → 0.813, 5.5 → 10.5 %), and 4 cameras almost nothing in matching (0.770 → 0.762, 1.4 → 1.2 %), only in the
  positions.
- **The correction recovers nearly all of it:** 2 cameras 0.691 / 11.8 % after two passes, 3 cameras 0.869 / 5.9 % after
  one, 4 cameras 0.778 / 1.2 %.
- **More cameras make the correction better.** With 2 cameras only the offset across the curves is seen; with 3 or 4,
  everything but a smooth deformation of the world. One pass recovers 0.69, 0.80 and 0.89 of the true field with 2,
  3 and 4 cameras.
- **Positions need more than the tracks.** No self-calibration can see a smooth deformation of the world, and the fitted
  field moves the reconstruction by a few µm (worse with each pass with 2 cameras). A metric reference (T0's wand: D1's
  alternative) fixes that.

**The shape model (M): stop.** No refit (a depth offset, separate focal offsets, separate blurs, a map over the image)
keeps more true pairs at the same random rate than S2's model. About a third of the widths' error is per particle, the
rest frame-to-frame noise: a model cannot remove either. M2 was not run.

**What it means for the library (your OK first):** an optional per-camera correction field (plan §4, D1) works and is
safe for matching, and helps most where the calibration error matters most: 2 or 3 cameras with errors near the
tolerance. For T1 it would buy about 11 % more real tracks and a fifth fewer ghosts at 1.0 px. If it is built:
- iterate two or three passes (the tracks under-report the error near the tolerance);
- fix the gauge with the wand data, so that it does not deform the reconstruction.

Without a library change, T1's deliverable could already be rerun on the twice-corrected frames at 1.0 px (this
plan's `m15vc2` run; it would replace X:'s, which needs your OK). The bigger lever for T1 remains a third camera (the
study: 3 cameras halve the ghosts).

## 2. What was built

**The estimator (`selfcal.py`).** A smooth correction field per camera, c(u, v) = (du, dv): where the camera's images
sit relative to where the model projects them (the disparity map of Wieneke 2008, D1).
- **Form:** per camera and component a tensor-product cubic B-spline over the sensor (10 x 6 intervals, about 130 px),
  with a bending-energy penalty whose weight is chosen by held-out loss, and a prior on the field's size (1 px).
- **The fit uses only what the cameras can see.** A track point seen by the cameras S has residuals r = d − P(X)
  (2|S| numbers) and a Jacobian J = dP/dX (2|S| x 3). Moving X changes r by −J dX, so only the part of r orthogonal
  to J's columns says anything about the cameras. The fit is least squares on that part (2|S| − 3 numbers per point,
  after whitening by the detections' noise): with 2 cameras one number, the offset across the epipolar curve; with 3
  cameras three; with 4 cameras five. One estimator serves 2, 3 and 4 cameras.
- **What stays as the model has it.** With 2 cameras, the part along the curves (depth) is unseen; the prior leaves it
  at 0. With 3–4 cameras only a smooth 3-D deformation of the world stays unseen (the gauge every self-calibration
  has).
- **Robust and weighted:** Cauchy weights on each point's projected residual (ghosts and blends count little); the
  detections' noise per axis sqrt(s_f² + (β L)²), L the box length along the axis, fitted from the residuals with
  s_f held at ≥ 0.15 px. On T1: s_f 0.37 px, β 0.073 (a 10 px line: 0.86 px along it).
- **The point selection (not VSC's, as you asked):** every point of every long track of at least 10 points, on every
  5th frame (T1) or every frame (synthetic), with a detection within 3 px of its projection in at least two cameras,
  paired one to one. No isolation test, no voxel cap, no all-cameras rule.
- **The dense field** holds the value of the nearest covered pixel beyond 48 px from the data.
- **The warp (V2c, V3):** W(q) = I(q + c(q)) by cubic-spline interpolation at the exact positions, on S1's float
  high-pass before the rounding (`cv2.remap` rounds positions to 1/32 px, so it is not used).

**Self-tests (`t0_selftest.py`, all pass):** B-spline identities; a known field recovered from synthetic detections with
T1's 2 cameras (observable error 0.013 px with 0.15 px noise) and the study's 4-camera rig (0.011 px), the unseen part
left at 0; the warp moves content by −c to 0.0003 px; on T1 the stored projections equal the cameras' (3e-5 px), and
the vectorised candidate pairs equal S2's exactly (22,171 pairs).

**Changes from the plan, each found while building:**
1. **The prior on the field's size.** Without it the 2-camera fit put 100 px into directions the data barely see
   (cam1's du enters the offset with a weight of about 0.002: the epipolar lines run 0.1° from horizontal).
2. **The robust loss and the noise floor** (`v2_diag.py`). On the 4-camera synthetic set the fitted noise fell to
   0.01 px (clean images give very precise centres for small images), the Huber fit then down-weighted 39 % of the
   points and invented 1 px fields in the barely seen directions. Of four variants, Cauchy weights with s_f ≥ 0.15 px
   recovered the truth best; an oracle (the same estimator on exact detections) recovers it to 0.03 px, so the
   estimator itself is sound. V0's T1 field was refitted with it (nearly the same: 0.283 against 0.279 px); the first
   fits are kept in `before_cauchy/`.
3. **The truth-free estimate of V0** needed a maximum-likelihood centre per region (below).
4. **M's held-out test was extended** to an ROC (true pairs kept at the same random rate) and an image-position term
   (M1b), so that a refit is judged fairly.
5. **The second pass (VSC's iteration)**, added after V2 showed that one pass recovers only part of the field (§5):
   on the 2-camera synthetic set, on Gallm and on T1 (`m15vc2`, at 1.0 px).
6. **A tolerance-only control on T1** (`m15t10`: the 15 px frames uncorrected at 1.0 px), so that V3's gain at 1.0 px
   can be told from the tighter tolerance's.
7. **Positions and the gauge** (`v2_positions.py`, `v2_gauge.py`), added when the 4-camera corrected run's x and z did
   not improve.

## 3. V0: the pattern on T1 (`v0_pattern.py`, `v0b_truthfree.py`)

**Data:** the 15 px run's 1,228 long tracks on every 5th frame (51 frames): 7,653 of their 22,686 points (34 %) have a
detection within 3 px in both cameras (1,078 tracks). The offset across the epipolar curve of these pairs: median
+0.08 px, robust SD 0.95 px.

**A real, stable pattern that an image field explains:**

| test (plan) | bar | T1 |
|---|---|---|
| regional medians of the epipolar offset (cam2 cells of 160 px), their RMS with the noise taken off | ≥ 0.3 px | **0.318 px** |
| the same map from frames 0–125 and 130–250 | correlation ≥ 0.7 | **0.80** |
| a truth-free estimate (all 1.1 M candidate pairs, no tracks) against the tracks' map, per 320 px region | agree | **correlation 0.97**, RMS difference 0.12 px (0.23 expected from their errors) |
| the offsets' dependence on depth, after the image field | stop if it exceeds the field | **none left** (0.000 px, noise taken off); the field 0.278 px |

- **Where it is.** The pattern is in cam2's dv: positive across the middle rows, negative at the top right and along the
  bottom (`v0_pattern.png`). cam1's maps are flat (0.05 px). du is near 0 in both cameras: the tracker's 3-D point
  absorbs the part along the curves, as expected.
- **Ghosts don't drive it.** The truth-free estimate uses no tracks: per region, the centre of the true pairs' peak in
  all candidate pairs' offsets, by maximum likelihood (a Gaussian on a sloped floor). T1-a's centroid method, the
  plan's first choice, is too noisy per 160 px cell (the true pairs stand about 1.7 σ above the floor per bin):
  correlation 0.06 there, 0.48 with the ML at 160 px, 0.97 at 320 px.
- **The field:** the offset it predicts at the track points has RMS 0.28 px; cam1 dv 0.29 px, cam2 dv 0.40 px RMS over
  the sensor (max 1.0 and 1.8 px). The two halves' fields predict the same offsets (correlation 0.84 and 0.77). It
  takes the points' offset from a robust SD of 0.73 to 0.59 px (null direction), and on held-out frames from 1.15 to
  0.96 (noise units).
- **A truth-free field** (EM on all candidate pairs: true pairs around the field's offset, random ones uniform) came out
  at 0.41x the tracks' field and sharpened held-out pairs less (V1; the mixture's spread on held-out frames 0.777 →
  0.717, against 0.697 with the tracks' field); the tracks' field is the one used. On Gallm, where the truth is known,
  it was worse too (37 % of the observable error explained against 54 %).

| cameras | no correction: position RMS x / y / z, reprojection | corrected (the smallest field) | corrected, the reconstruction's affine moves held (weight 1e7) |
|---|---|---|---|

- **The correction makes the images consistent** (the reprojection halves) **but can move the reconstruction.** The part
  of an error field that a smooth deformation of the world explains is invisible to the tracks. The fit fills it with the
  smallest field, which is not the deformation-free one, so the triangulated points move by a few µm.
- **Holding each point still** (penalising its triangulation's move) undoes the consistency gain: per-camera fields
  cannot realise the correction without moving some points (2 cameras, weight 1: reprojection 0.21 px).
- **Holding only the affine part** (shift, turn, scale, shear: 12 numbers) keeps the consistency. With 4 cameras the
  positions then end better than with no correction (3.6 / 3.1 / 5.7 µm), with 3 partly, with 2 not (the move there is
  not affine). Stronger weights trade consistency again.
- **For matching this does not matter** (it uses the consistency only); the tracking runs above used the smallest field.
  For positions, a metric reference fixes the gauge: T0's wand (D1's alternative: refit with the wand data and the
  tracks together), or a known target.

## 8. Limitations

- **One field per camera, in image space.** It absorbs any error that a camera's image position explains (the tube's
  position, lens distortion, a detector bias that varies over the sensor). An error that depends on depth at one
  image position is not taken up; V0 found none left on T1 after the field (0.000 px of depth trend).
- **The tracks under-report the error** where it nears the matching tolerance (a selection by the tolerance: one pass
  recovers 0.58–0.89 of the field). Two passes were run; more would recover more. A truncation-aware fit (the tracked
  pairs' offsets as a normal truncated at the tolerance) could do it in one.
- **The gauge** (a smooth deformation of the world) is unseen; the fit leaves the smallest field there, which moves
  the reconstruction by a few µm (worse with each pass, with 2 cameras). Matching is unaffected; positions need a metric
  reference.
- **The warp interpolates** (cubic spline): it costs 2.5 % of the true pairs within 3 px on T1 and lowers the measured
  noise by 3 %. On Gallm, the warped and the re-rendered sets track alike (0.143 / 22.5 % and 0.141 / 24.3 % at 1.5 px).
- **T1 has no truth.** Its ghost estimates read 2–3x the truth (the study's G3); they are compared between runs.
- **The synthetic error field is an image field** (T0's wand-residual pattern), the kind the correction can represent;
  cam3's and cam4's are cam2's and cam1's turned by 180°.
- **Shared cores.** The runs shared the 32 logical cores two at a time (12 threads each), with another session's job
  for part of the time; the s per frame compare only roughly.
