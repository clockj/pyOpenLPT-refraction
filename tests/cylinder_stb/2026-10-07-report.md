# Cylinder STB: tracking tracers through refractive cylinder windows — library report

7 October 2026. Plan: `cases/cyl_20261007/2026-10-07-plan.md`, Phases 1–5 (approved with all phases). The case
itself (T0's calibration, tracking T1) is reported in `cases/cyl_20261007/2026-10-07-report.md`. Every number
below is read from an output beside its script in this folder, on the final build `p4c` unless a build is named.

## 1. In short

You asked for STB to track tracers inside a refractive cylinder, with two concerns:
- the images are stretched, so the Gaussian fit may fail;
- a particle may have no image or several images in a camera. Your rule: keep a camera only if exactly one image
  falls on its sensor, otherwise ignore the particle on that camera.

What the library now does:

| need | what was built | result |
|---|---|---|
| projection fast enough for STB | a warm-started projection from a nearby point's ray (Phase 1) | 2.3 µs per call against 185–222 µs; STB on a tube case 12× faster with the same tracks |
| no-image / multi-image regions | per-camera image statuses implementing your rule, a single-image certificate, Shake and residuals made consistent with them (Phase 2) | statuses agree with the full search on 50,000 of 50,000 points |
| matching through the tube | curved epipolar lines (Phase 3) | the true image within 0.018 px of the curve (straight lines: up to 1.04 px); 4× fewer false candidates at T1's density |
| stretched images | an OTF "segment" model (a line ⊗ Gaussian), ROIs from it, a line detector (C++ port of the case's prototype), and a depth cue from the image's shape (Phases 2 and 4) | line images removed from the residual ≥ 98.8 % (today 12–61 %) |
| end to end (synthetic, T1's density, two cameras) | all of the above | recall 0.16 → 0.80, ghosts 92.5 % → 6.4 %, position error 39–52 → 11–14 µm, 5.3 s per frame against today's 23.6 s |

**Defaults are unchanged.** Existing PINPLATE and PINHOLE cases track bit-identically to HEAD, with the Python
module and with OpenLPT.exe. A PINCYL case also tracks bit-identically when the two new switches are off. Everything
new is behind config keys or applies only to PINCYL cameras.

**Not met, explained in section 6:**
- F3 path B: 1e-5 px at the near/far junction, which is rounding of the grazing ray;
- L5's literal criterion: the prototype itself does not meet it under 1e-9 noise;
- R1 ≠ R0 bit-for-bit on the line case: tracks equal statistically, not byte for byte.

## 2. What changed

### Phase 1 — fast projection

- **`RefractCurved`:**
  - `projectCylinderFrom(stack, pinhole, point, lam0, cz0, far0, tol, itmax)`: Newton on the cylinder's equations,
    started from a nearby point's ray invariants (Λ, C_z, near/far). It works in unit-scaled coordinates
    (u = 2^⌊log2 r_P⌋, exact) and stops at min(1e-13, tol). It returns nothing when it fails or leaves the domain,
    and the caller then falls back to the full search.
  - `singleImage(stack, r_p, r_x, dz)`: the certificate that a point has exactly one image (at most one densifying
    interface, r_X < r_c(0), |dz| < Z0(C1)).
- **`Camera`:**
  - `ProjectionHint {lambda, c_z, is_far, valid}`, `projectWithHint`, `usesProjectionHint`,
    `hintFromLineOfSight`;
  - the cylinder camera implements them;
  - `OPENLPT_PROJECTION_HINTS=0` turns them off.
- **`Object3D::_proj_hint`** (one per camera) travels with the object. `projectObject2D` uses it, and
  `StereoMatch::triangulateMatch` sets it from the lines of sight.

### Phase 2 — per-camera visibility, Shake, ROIs

- **Statuses:** `Camera::multiValued`, `provablySingleImage(pt)` and `imageStatus(pt, margin_px, merge_px)` →
  None / One / Ambiguous. This is your rule: One means exactly one image on the sensor (within a margin), and
  images closer than `merge_px` count as one.
- **Where the statuses act:** a camera whose status is not One is left out of that particle in
  `isReconstructable` and `TracerShakeStrategy::selectShakeCam`. Shake gives a multi-valued camera an
  empty-prediction cost, and the residual image subtracts every image of an uncertified particle.
- **OTF model 1, "segment":** parameters (a, b, c, alpha, sigma), with profile
  g(t; L) = [Φ((t+L/2)/σ) − Φ((t−L/2)/σ)] / [2Φ(L/2σ) − 1] along the image's axes, turned by alpha. It has
  `OTF::intensity` and `halfExtent`, and an optional "segment" section in the OTF file.
- **Tracer keys:** `otf_file` (load a table instead of estimating it), `roi_from_otf` and `roi_sigma` (regions
  from the OTF's extent).

### Phase 3 — curved epipolar lines

- **The curve:** `Camera::lineImagesCurve`, `clipToModel`, and `StereoMatch::makeCurve2DFromLOS3D`. It builds a
  line of sight's image as a polyline: clipped to the view box and to the water, 8 segments, then subdivided until
  the curve is within 0.05 px.
- **Matching on it:** `ImageCurve` and IDMap strip visiting along polylines, `dist2ToPolyline`, and
  `checkBackProjection` with polylines.
- **Switch:** `OPENLPT_CURVED_LINES=0` draws the lines straight, as before.

### Phase 4 — line images

- **The detector:** `LineFinder` (new: `inc/libObject/LineFinder.h`, `src/srcObject/LineFinder.cpp`) is the C++
  port of the case's prototype `lines.py`:
  - matched filters (separable, robust noise), 3×3 maxima and box non-maximum suppression;
  - a Levenberg–Marquardt fit per object of A·P(x; Lx, sx)·P(y; Ly, sy) + c, with the clipped-noise expectation;
  - deblending passes, a second pass on the residual, and validation.
- **Keys:** tracer key `detector` ("peaks", the default, or "lines"), and `lines` (every `LineFinderParam`
  field, and `noise` as one number or one per camera).
- **Shape fields:** `Tracer2D` carries the detection's shape (`_has_shape`, `_len_h_px`, `_len_v_px`,
  `_sigma_h_px`, `_sigma_v_px`, `_sigma_px`, `_amp`).
- **The depth cue, `shape_check`:** in stereo matching, each detection's RMS widths √(L²/12 + s²) must agree with
  the OTF's at the triangulated point. The tolerance is max(`shape_tol_px`, `shape_tol_rel` × predicted), with
  defaults 0.8 px and 0.25. Widths, not lengths, because a short image's length and blur trade against each
  other while the width is well determined.
- **Threads:** IPR runs its per-camera detection serially when the detector is "lines".

## 4. Phase 5: synthetic end-to-end

`r5_ablation_out.txt` and `.json`. The case `data/r5_lines` (`synth_lines.py`):
- the 6 Oct T2 cameras (two PINCYL);
- 3,000 particles in the water, 40 frames, about 943 visible to both cameras per frame;
- line images rendered physically from the shape model (A 15 mm, σ0 1 px), with noise 7.

Scored against the truth by `eval_truth.py`:

| run | build | what | recall | ghosts per frame | position RMS (x, y, z) | long tracks (ghost) | track purity | s per frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | head1007 | today's code | 0.162 | 2,398 (92.5 %) | 45, 39, 52 µm | 3,316 (2,836) | 0.074 | 23.6 |
| R1 | p1 | + projection hints | 0.165 | 2,411 (92.5 %) | 46, 39, 52 µm | 3,350 (2,861) | 0.075 | 1.31 |
| R2 | p123 | + statuses, curved lines, the true OTF and its ROIs | 0.156 | 2,239 (92.0 %) | 48, 19, 52 µm | 3,337 (2,877) | 0.083 | 1.74 |
| R3 | p4c | + line detector and depth cue | **0.796** | **51 (6.4 %)** | **11, 12, 14 µm** | 930 (38) | **0.932** | 5.25 |

The plan's criteria:
- **R3 against R0:** fewer ghosts and a higher recall. **Met**, by far.
- **R1 equals R0:** met statistically, not to the byte (section 6).

**The line detector and the depth cue are what matter here.** With today's peak finder (R0–R2), a line image
yields several peaks. Two cameras cannot then tell true matches from ghosts, and over 90 % of the reconstructed
particles are ghosts.

R3 was rerun on `p4c` after two changes:
- the depth cue compares RMS widths;
- the true OTF table stores box lengths of equal second moment, (√3/2)·L (`synth_lines.py --otf-only`).

The development build `p4dev` (lengths) had recall 0.601 with the same ghost share.

## 6. What was not met, and why

- **F3 path B, 1.0e-5 and 2.0e-5 px at the near/far junction.** The worst points sit exactly at the junction,
  where the ray grazes (δ ≈ 1e-16 of the domain edge). There the projection can only be evaluated to about
  eps/√δ ≈ 2e-8 in Φ, which with the long lens is 1e-5 px (F3b). It is rounding, not a bug. Following your rule for
  rounding-level effects, it is documented, not fixed.
- **L5: "centres within 1e-3 px" is unattainable for any implementation of this detector on crowded frames.**
  - On isolated objects the port equals the prototype to 5e-6 px (L5b step 2).
  - On T1's frames, 3–5 % of objects are blended. Their fits sit in flat valleys with several optima. The C++ and
    Python fits land in different ones, with χ² ratios from 0.74 to 1.68 and a median of 1.000 (step 3).
  - The prototype does the same against itself: adding 1e-9 noise to a frame moves 3.2 % of its centres by more
    than 1e-3 px, up to 0.24 px (step 4).
  - The port is therefore as close to the prototype as the prototype is to itself.
- **R1 ≠ R0 to the byte on the line case** (2 of 11 files identical). The hints change projections by ≤ 1e-6 px
  (F1). On a case with 92 % ghosts, that is enough to flip threshold decisions in STB, which then diverge. The
  scores are the same (recall 0.165 against 0.162, ghosts 92.5 % in both). On the spot case (F5) the tracks are
  identical to 1e-6 mm, and with `OPENLPT_PROJECTION_HINTS=0` the code is today's path exactly.

## 7. Using it

A tracer through the tube (config.json, inside the object's `tracer`):

```json
"tracer": {
  "intensity_threshold": 28, "radius_px": 2.0,
  "detector": "lines", "lines": {"noise": [8.06, 5.92]},
  "otf_file": "otf_shape.txt", "roi_from_otf": true, "roi_sigma": 3.0,
  "shape_check": true, "shape_tol_px": 0.8, "shape_tol_rel": 0.2
}
```

- **The images** must be preprocessed as the case's S1 does: particles bright on a background of 0, 8 bits.
- **`noise`** is each camera's noise in those counts.
- **`otf_file`** is a segment-model table. The case's `tests/s2_shape_fit.py` fits one from the recording itself,
  and `shape_model.build_otf` writes one from known parameters.
- **What applies by itself:** the hints, the statuses and the curved lines apply to PINCYL cameras without any
  key.
