## 1. Result

Several cameras that look through one water-filled tube can be calibrated from wand frames alone, focal lengths
included, with no camera input at all.

- **From scratch, without hints, all 50 random rigs** (2-4 cameras, 0.4-1.5 m) reached the global minimum, i.e. the
  answer of the same fit started at the truth (T5):
  - RMS identical to 7e-14 px;
  - every parameter within 3.6e-5 sigma;
  - every observation kept.
- **f comes from the wand** to 0.02-0.28 % (Cramer-Rao) on every rig studied. On the reference rig (three cam1
  replicas, 500 frames) it is 0.064 % (T2).
- **The reported uncertainties are the actual scatter** (T4, 20 noisy runs):
  - pooled z-scores: mean +0.08, std 0.93;
  - whitened chi-square: 19.3 for 19 parameters.
- **The cameras agree on unseen points to 0.008 px RMS** (gauge-free). Held-out wand lengths scatter 2.4 um on a 10 mm
  wand. Both are within 3 % of the prediction (T7).
- **Bad data:**
  - gross outliers and A/B swaps are all rejected, with the answer within 0.8 sigma of the clean data's;
  - observations whose point has a close double image near the wall are all dropped (T8).
- **With the wand length left free, the tube's radii fix the scale to 0.02 %.** That makes the L-free fit a sharp check
  of the tube's constants and the wand length (T9).
- **The camFiles are pyOpenLPT PINCYL files** and reload bit-identically (T10).

Nothing in `paper/`, the C++ library, `pose_calibrator.py`, the GUI wand modules or the case folder was changed.
Nothing was committed. pyOpenLPT's full test suite gives 237 passed, 7 skipped, and only its known failures: one test,
and two modules that fail to import (listed in its CLAUDE.md).

| `tests/test_cylinder_wand.py` (new) | pytest subset: 6 fast tests (under 3 s), 1 slow (from scratch, about 25 s) |
| `tests/cylinder_wand/` | Plan, scripts T0-T10 and diagnostics, their outputs, the reports, `run_all.py` |
| `.gitignore` | Negations for the two test paths |

Use:

```python
import cylinder_wand as cw, cylinder_wand_io as cio      # (package: modules.camera_calibration.curved_calibration)
tube = cw.cm.Tube(radii=(100.80625, 97.63125), n=(1.0, 1.49, 1.333))
data = cw.WandData(uv, 10.0, sizes)       # uv[frame, camera, end A/B, (u, v)], NaN where not seen
sol = cw.calibrate(data, tube)            # options: hints={"f_range": (lo, hi)}, fit_L=True, fit_k1=True, guard_px=30
cio.write_result(sol, "out", names=["cam1", "cam2", "cam3"], pixel_mm=[0.02] * 3)
```

The output frame is the tube frame:
- the axis is Y through the origin;
- Z points towards camera 1;
- the origin sits at the wand's mean height.

The camFiles carry that axis.

  - beyond the fold: 19,429 starts, no false solution.

  - sigma(f)/f = 0.064 %;

- **T2, rig guidance:**
  1. cover depth: +-20 mm or more as seen from each camera (+-40 mm: f to 0.020 %; +-10 mm: 0.164 %);
  2. 3 or more cameras, 45-120 deg apart;
  3. 500 or more frames (sigma falls as 1/sqrt(N));

- **Start models.** The best pinhole with fx = fy misses by 96-100 px. The best fx != fy pinhole runs off to infinite

## 4. Implementation notes; defects found and fixed by the tests

**Forward projection.**
- Newton solves in undistorted normalised coordinates. The pixel then follows in closed form through the distortion, so
  f, cx, cy and k have exact derivatives.
- Found by T0:
  - a false convergence on a grazing edge, where the ray's derivatives blow up and the step vanished 5.9 mm off the
    ray. Convergence now also requires the ray to pass through the point;
  - starts whose ray misses the tube. These are moved by bisection towards a ray aimed at the axis;
  - Newton now also backtracks on the offset.

**A discrete symmetry the plan missed.**
- A half-turn about Z (X -> -X, Y -> -Y) maps the tube onto itself and keeps camera 1's centre in place. The gauge
  leaves it free: it is the sign of the axis direction.
- `canonical()` fixes it: +Y up in camera 1's image, or right if the axis is nearer the image's horizontal.
- Found by T5 (a portrait camera 1).

**The start** (all found by T5, rig 37: cameras 0.6-1.3 m, deep cloud):
- **Candidates are ranked after a quick stage-3 fit, not as placed.** With the affine rotations still about 1 deg off,
  near-telecentric placements 5-6 m away scored better than the right one. Stage 3 then stalled there at 4.8 px:
  f 4-6x too large, cameras metres too far.
  - Stage 4 still found the right minimum, but see the next item.
  - The plan had asked for exactly this scoring; the shortcut was mine.
- **Distance and f from the affine stretch, like for like.** The model camera projects the reconstructed cloud, an
  affine camera is fitted to those images, and its stretch ratio and magnification are matched to the observed ones.
  The local ratio at the centroid was biased towards far.

**The outlier loop, made correct in two steps:**
1. **Rejection is no longer one-way.** On rig 37 a first round from a poor state rejected 50 good observations for
   good, and the answer moved 0.24 sigma. Every round now chooses the active set afresh from all usable observations:
   - frames without active rows are re-triangulated before they are judged;
   - re-admission needs 0.8 of the limit (hysteresis).
2. **Mahalanobis residuals, not |r|.**
   - With two cameras a frame has 3 residual dof over 8 coordinates, so each residual is nearly one-dimensional and
     shrunk to about 0.6 sigma. A 5-sigma Rayleigh cut on |r| rejected about 0.4 % of good observations, 4-12 per rig
     (whole frames, via the two-camera rule). The robust sigma came out 0.054 px for 0.1 px noise.
   - Each residual is now judged under its own predicted covariance from its frame's 5 parameters: sigma^2 (I - P)
     when it is in the fit, sigma^2 (I + P) when it is predicted from the others.
   - d^2 / sigma^2 is then chi-square with 2 dof on every rig: the robust sigma reads 0.097-0.101 px, and no T5 rig
     loses an observation.

**Multi-image guard.**
- Only points beyond the fold radius are checked. The census is also taken 20 um away along each axis, because next to
  the fold a second image appears or vanishes within microns (found by T8).
- Close double images are rare: just beyond the fold, 6 in 9000 (point, camera) pairs are within 30 px. Elsewhere the
  two images are typically hundreds of px apart, so a second image is a separate blob, not a blend.

**Levenberg-Marquardt:**
- **It stops on the step size, not the cost decrease.** Along the near-telecentric f-distance direction a 1e-14
  relative decrease still left the estimate 1e-5 sigma short (T6).
- **Its Schur ridge is 1e-15,** not 1e-12; the larger one broke the stabiliser's exact null space (T6).

**The S^2 update** is the sphere's exponential map, w <- cos|d| w + sin|d| d/|d|. The plan wrote the equivalent
rotation form.

## 5. Criteria restated (honest accounting)

1. **T0 rays.**
   - The plan's 1e-9 mm is below the C++'s own round-off for a distant camera. `lineOfSight` forms the direction as the
     difference of two points about 1 m from the origin and 1 mm apart, which costs about 1e-13 rad (emulated in
     `t0b_cpp_direction_roundoff.py`).
   - Restated as the C++ ray reprojected by the fast model: 1e-8 px. Measured 1.0e-9 px.
2. **T2 gate.**
   - "At most 0.05 px RMS added to the projection of unseen points" did not name a frame.
   - Gauge-free (re-triangulated), which is what triangulation and reprojection in tracking use: 0.0077 px, **GO**.
   - In the tube frame modulo its stabiliser: 0.62 px. This **fails** the literal limit. 90 % of it is where the tube
     sits across the axis relative to the rig: sigma 12.8 um, tilt 13 arcsec (`t2b_error_modes.py`).
   - In the camera-1 gauge: 4.2 px, the unobservable slide along the axis.
   - I proceeded on the gauge-free reading and report the axis position as its own figure. For scale, the real
     single-camera plate case placed the axis to +-0.135 mm.
3. **T6 camera order:** in sigma units (1e-4 sigma) rather than 1e-6 mm, because the cost is so flat along the
   f-distance direction.
4. **T7:** the gauge-free and held-out ratios are gated (0.8-1.2x). The gauge-dependent ones are reported.
5. **T8 (b):** judged against the truth, because the added near-wall frames carry information; the 400-frame fit is
   itself uncertain. Frames with close double images were planted just beyond the fold radius, where they occur.

## 6. Not done, and limits

- **Out of scope for this plan (user, 2026-10-01):** real data, the GUI's wand CSV reader, and GUI integration.
- **The start needs 12 or more frames seen whole by every camera,** and factorises only those. With little common
  view, for example 4 cameras that never all see one frame, it would need a pairwise extension.
- **k1 (`fit_k1=True`)** is tested through its Jacobian only (T1b), not in a full calibration.
- **The synthetic data have no model error.** The real case found an image-fixed 0.1-0.2 px pattern that PINCYL cannot
  carry; the first real wand data set will show what it does to a wand calibration.
- **Run time:** about 20-60 s per calibration from scratch (500 frames, 2-4 cameras). Most of it is the candidate search
  (about 90 candidates, each with a quick fit).
