What the cylinder wand-calibration tests show

Every number below comes from synthetic data. Wand detections were generated by pyOpenLPT's C++ tube camera (PINCYL) for your case's tube: outer radius 100.8 mm, 3.2 mm acrylic wall, water inside. Each detection has 0.1 px of noise per coordinate, and the wand is 10 mm long. The calibration receives only the detections, the wand length, the image sizes and the tube. It gets no camera positions and no focal lengths.

01How many cameras

    
Every calibration test used rigs of two, three or four cameras. Most tests use the reference rig: three copies of your cam1 (f = 41 459 px at 1094 mm from the axis), 45° apart. T5 adds 50 random rigs, and T2 compares 18 rig designs without fitting. The code accepts any number of cameras, but no rig with five or more has been tested.

    

    
    
  


  

    
02Calibration from scratch (T5)

    
Each random rig was calibrated twice: once from nothing, and once started at the true cameras, which finds the best answer the data allow. The run from nothing begins with the bootstrap (stages 1–2) and then refines in stages 3–4. All 50 runs from nothing reached the same answer as the runs from the truth. The largest parameter difference was 3.6×10⁻⁵ of that parameter's σ, and the RMS agreed to 7×10⁻¹⁴ px. No good observation was rejected.

    
  


  

    
03Precision and rig design (T2)

    
These are standard deviations at 0.1 px noise, computed from the information matrix without fitting (the Cramér–Rao bound). T4 confirms that real fits reach them. Each design changes one thing from the reference rig; the last group combines changes. The depth the wand covers matters most for f, followed by the camera distance and the number of frames. The angle between cameras matters little once it is 45° or more.

    
  


  

    
04Are the error bars honest? (T4, T7)

    
T4 calibrates the reference rig 20 times, each time with a new wand path and new noise, and compares each error with the σ the calibration predicts. If the σ are right, the 380 z-scores (19 parameters × 20 fits) follow a standard normal distribution. T7 compares predicted and actual errors on data the fit never saw.

06Wrong tube constants (T9)

    
Here the data were made with the true tube and calibrated with one constant wrong. The reprojection RMS barely moves, so the residuals cannot reveal a wrong constant. Letting the wand length float can: the tube's radii fix the scale to 0.02 %, so the fitted length moves away from the nominal by 5–33σ. The last column shows what the wrong constant costs a tracking run.

    
  


  

    
07Detection noise (T11)

    
Every test above used 0.1 px of Gaussian noise. T11 varies the noise in four ways:

    
    
      
  • T11a: 40 data sets at each of seven noise levels on the reference rig, each fitted from a start near the truth.
    • 
      
  • T11b: calibration from nothing at up to 5 px.
    • 
      
  • T11c and T11d: noise that is not uniform Gaussian, either a camera or wand end noisier than the rest, or occasional large errors.
    • 
      
  • T11e: a fixed error pattern on each image, of the kind your real cam1 case showed.
    • 
    

    
The short answer: every error grows in proportion to the noise, the error bars stay honest at every level, and the start from nothing holds up to 2 px on every rig tried. The one serious risk is a fixed pattern on the images, not random noise.
