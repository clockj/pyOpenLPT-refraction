## 1. The question, and the short answer

Can pyOpenLPT calibrate several cameras that look through one water-filled tube from wand frames alone, focal lengths
included, and deliver PINCYL camera files? **Yes, I expect so.** One risk, observability, is checked first (Phase 0,
test T2).

- **Every physical piece exists and is validated.**
  - The C++ PINCYL camera: forward projection with every image, and closed-form back-projection. The two agree to
    about 1e-11 mm (worst 7e-8 mm).
  - A calibration through this tube on real data: the cam1 case fits 0.20 px and places the axis to 0.14 mm from one
    camera.
  - A wand fit with the port given: `PoseCalibrator.fit_wand`, on synthetic data.
- **The tube is a strong lens, which is what makes this possible.**
  - Read-only probe on cam1's calibrated camera (scratchpad `cyl_aniso_probe.py`):

    | point depth from the axis, towards the camera | across the axis | along the axis | ratio |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | -20 mm | 52.68 px/mm | 38.26 px/mm | 1.377 |
    | 0 | 50.52 px/mm (x1.333 the no-tube f/D) | 38.80 px/mm (x1.024) | 1.302 |
    | +20 mm | 48.53 px/mm | 39.35 px/mm | 1.233 |

  - Across the axis, the water magnifies by n_water = 1.333 at the axis. Along it, the wall acts as a flat window.
    Both were checked (section 9).
  - Across the axis, the scale *falls* as a point comes towards the camera: -7.9 % over these 40 mm, against +2.8 %
    along the axis.
  - Where the tube sits relative to each camera, and how far away each camera is, is therefore written into every
    wand image to first order. Through a flat window it is not.
- **What does not exist is the path from raw wand detections to the answer:**
  - the tube's pose relative to the cameras is unknown;
  - the cameras start from nothing;
  - the existing wand fit needs a start near the answer;
  - it is slow: it makes about 6,000 single-ray C++ calls per residual evaluation for 1,000 frames and 3 cameras;
  - it minimises a ray residual, not the reprojection error.

## 3. The problem

### 3.1 Unknowns and knowns

- **Known:**
  - the tube: R_outer, R_inner, n_air, n_acrylic, n_water;
  - the wand length L;
  - image size and principal point (held at the image centre).
- **Per camera, estimated from the wand:** pose (6) and f (1). No board calibration is assumed. k1 is optional and
  off by default; the case rejected it.
- **Per frame:** the two endpoints, 5 dof: midpoint (3) and direction (2), with |X_A - X_B| = L held exactly.
- **The tube's pose relative to the cameras:** absorbed by the frame (section 3.2).

**Where f comes from.**
- Each camera's image scale fixes f / distance; how the scale changes with depth separates the two.
- A wand point's depth along one camera's line of sight is seen sideways by the other cameras.
- f is therefore determined by the depth the wand covers, the angle between cameras and the number of frames.
- Through the tube there is a second, stronger depth cue: the transverse scale changes about 3x faster with depth
  than the axial one (section 1).
- T2 measures how well f is determined, and on which rigs.
- For the projection of points inside the calibrated volume, an f / distance pair that trades along the
  near-telecentric direction costs little. Extrapolation beyond the volume is where an error in f shows.

### 3.2 Frame and gauge

- **Tube frame.** Origin on the axis, Y along the axis, Z towards camera 1. This is the case's convention: Y up the
  tube, Z towards the camera. The tube is then fixed and has no parameters, and all the geometry is in the cameras.
- **Gauge.** What stays free is the tube's stabiliser, **2 dof**: spin about the axis and slide along it.
  - The earlier synthetic work recorded this, and it showed that an unfixed fit drifts along that orbit to rms 0 at
    poses degrees wrong.
  - Here it is fixed by convention, not by projection onto a reference. Camera 1's centre is held at azimuth 0
    (X = 0) and height Y = 0, with its distance from the axis free.
  - That removes the null space exactly, so the covariance is the Cramer-Rao bound without a pseudo-inverse.
  - After the fit, the origin slides along Y to the wand points' mean height. That is a relabelling and changes no
    residual.
- **Scale is fixed twice:** by L and by the tube's radii. Through a flat window it comes almost only from the wand.
  - A fit with L free is therefore a check of the tube constants (stage 5).

### 3.3 Why not pinhole first, as the plate pipeline does

- **Through the tube, a narrow-field camera's image is affine to first order, with anisotropic magnification**
  (section 1). A 10 mm wand at cam1's distance is about 390 px long along the axis and about 505 px across it.
- **The pinhole wand calibrator holds fx = fy.** It cannot represent that 115 px difference.
- **Even fx != fy leaves 11.4 px RMS** on the real cam1 data (case T5), because the transverse scale changes with
  depth the wrong way for a pinhole.
- **An affine camera holds the anisotropy exactly and needs no start:**
  - factorise the wand detections (Tomasi-Kanade);
  - upgrade to metric with the wand itself: every frame's segment d has length L, so d^T Q d = L^2, linear in the 6
    entries of Q;
  - cam1-like cameras (+-0.9 deg field) are near-telecentric. That is where an affine model is *best*, and where the
    pinhole path's essential-matrix start is worst.
- **Expected affine model error:** about 10 px over a +-20 mm volume, because of the depth change in the table of
  section 1. That should be inside the basin of the refractive fit; T5 checks it.

### 3.4 Inputs: no guess of the camera positions is needed

- **Required:**
  - the tube's radii and indices;
  - the wand length;
  - each camera's image size, plus its pixel size, used only to report f in mm;
  - the detections, with A and B labelled.
- **Not required:** camera positions, orientations or focal lengths. The start comes from the data (stages 1-2).
- **Optional hints** narrow the start's search and are never needed for it to work:
  - an f range;
  - approximate camera azimuths or distances;
  - an existing camera file.
- **T5 tests the start without any hint,** on random rigs; with hints is the secondary case.

## 4. Algorithm

**Stage 0: data.**
- Keep frames with both endpoints seen in at least 2 cameras.
- Gross outliers are flagged by the wand length of a quick triangulation.

**Stage 1: affine bootstrap. No input.**
- Factorise the frames seen by every camera into affine cameras and points (rank-3 SVD), then upgrade to metric with
  L. The metric frame is then known up to a rotation and a mirror image (both checked, section 9).
- Each affine camera splits (polar decomposition) into:
  - an orientation: the camera's image axes in the reconstruction;
  - a 2x2 stretch: its two principal magnifications. The larger is across the tube, the smaller along it.
- Carry both mirror candidates: a narrow-field image cannot tell a scene from its mirror image (Necker reversal).
  Perspective and refraction tell them apart later.

**Stage 2: placing the tube. No input.**
- **Axis direction:** each camera's low-magnification image direction, combined over the cameras. For each camera,
  the axis lies in the plane spanned by that direction and the camera's viewing direction; two such planes meet in
  the axis direction.
- **Axis position** (2 numbers):
  - start where the cameras' central rays meet in the cross-section;
  - then a coarse grid, inside the region where every wand point lies within R_inner;
  - how much of the grid is needed is measured in T5.
- **Camera distances, and so f:** the affine magnification fixes f / distance. A 1-D search over the distance
  (log-spaced, 0.3-3 m) sets them. In the case, f held at the lens's nominal 9000 px fitted 8.4 px instead of
  0.2 px, so a wrong distance is plainly visible.
- **Scoring:** each candidate (axis position x mirror x distance) is scored by a few iterations of stage 3, and the
  best is kept.

**Stage 3: coarse joint fit.**
- Back-projection residual, always defined.
- Points eliminated by triangulation; soft wand length; the 2x2 whitening; robust loss.
- This is `fit_wand`'s formulation, moved to the fast model, with f free and the tube-frame gauge.

**Stage 4: maximum-likelihood bundle adjustment.**
- **Residual:** the reprojection error in px.
  - The forward projection is the image nearest the detection, by Newton on the closed-form back-projection (the
    case's method).
  - Its derivatives follow by implicit differentiation from the 2x2 pixel Jacobian that Newton already has. They
    cost no more than the back-projection's.
- **Unknowns:** explicit endpoints, 5 dof per frame, with the wand length exact.
- **Solver:** Levenberg-Marquardt on the manifold of section 5, with the frame blocks eliminated.
- **Outliers:** 5 robust sigma, repeated until there are none (the case's rule).
- **Multi-image guard:** an observation whose point has a second image near the detection is dropped. The earlier
  work found such observations rare and biased.
  - Points farther than r_c = 75.6 mm from this tube's axis can have two images or none.
  - A wand near the axis is far inside that radius.
- **Covariance:** of the camera parameters, marginalised over the points.

**Stage 5: checks and outputs.**
- **Per camera:** reprojection RMS and the residual map.
- **Wand length from free triangulation** (no constraint): mean and RMS. This is the usual wand figure.
- **Held-out frames** (k-fold): their reprojection error and wand length.
- **L free:** the fitted L against the nominal, which tests the tube constants against the wand.
- **The Cramer-Rao sigmas** and the largest correlations.
- **Files:**
  - one PINCYL camFile per camera, written and re-loaded through the C++;
  - `calibration_result.json`;
  - a short text report.

## 5. The optimiser (Lie groups)

The unknowns live on curved spaces. Each iteration solves for a small step in flat local coordinates at the current
estimate, then maps the step back:

| unknown | space | update |
|---|---|---|
| camera rotation R (world to camera) | SO(3) | R <- R exp([dw]x): a small turn about the camera's own centre |
| camera centre C | R^3 | C <- C + dC. Camera 1 moves only towards or away from the axis, which is the gauge. |
| focal length f | R+ (multiplicative group) | f <- f exp(dl): the step is relative, and f stays positive |
| wand midpoint m | R^3 | m <- m + dm |
| wand direction w | unit sphere S^2 | w <- exp([a t1 + b t2]x) w, with t1, t2 perpendicular to w: 2 dof |

- The endpoints are X_A, X_B = m +- (L/2) w, so the wand length is exact.
- **Re-anchoring.** After every iteration the estimate becomes the new anchor and the steps restart at zero. The
  local coordinates are therefore only ever used near zero, where they are accurate and have no singularity. An
  additive Rodrigues vector breaks down near 180 deg, which is where cameras ringing a tube sit.
- **Not SE(3).** SE(3) is the same idea for a rotation plus a translation, but it is normally applied to the
  world-to-camera transform (R, t).
  - There, a rotation step swings the camera's *position* about the world origin, about 1.1 m away here, unless the
    step is written in the camera frame.
  - With (R, C), a rotation turns the camera about its pinhole and a translation moves it in the tube's frame.
  - The gauge (camera 1 at X = Y = 0) is then a condition on C directly, and the reported sigmas are in mm and
    degrees.
  - This is the same choice as `pose_calibrator.py`'s `Pose.retract` and the case's `cylfit`.
- **Levenberg-Marquardt.**
  - Normal equations J^T J + lambda D, where D is the diagonal of J^T J.
  - Each frame's 5 parameters touch only that frame's observations. The 5x5 frame blocks are therefore eliminated
    (Schur complement), leaving a dense camera system: 19 unknowns for 3 cameras, 26 for 4.
  - The cost is linear in the number of frames.
- **Covariance.** The inverse of the reduced camera system, times the residual variance, is the Cramer-Rao bound on
  the camera parameters. T4 checks it against the actual scatter.

## 10. Risks

- **Observability of f.** Near-telecentric cameras make f and distance nearly interchangeable for one camera. The
  wand's depth comes from the other cameras, so the angle between cameras and the depth the wand covers matter.
  T2 measures it before any library code exists, and turns it into rig guidance.
- **The start without input.** The affine start has about 10 px of model error, and the tube's position starts
  unknown. T5 measures the success rate on random rigs.
- **Tube constants.** n_water and R move the absolute geometry, as in the case's T7. T9 quantifies this, and the
  L-free check detects it.
- **Model error near the wall.** The case found an image-fixed residual pattern of 0.1-0.2 px that PINCYL cannot
  carry. It is absent from synthetic data, so the synthetic tests do not see it. Noted for the first real data set.
