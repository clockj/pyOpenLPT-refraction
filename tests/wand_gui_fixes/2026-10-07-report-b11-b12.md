## 1. In short

- **B11: the Pinhole model calibrates cameras of different image sizes.**
  - **What changed:** each camera's principal point is bounded around its own image centre, where its start already
    was.
  - **The test:** a camera of 1024 × 768 px among cameras of 1280 × 800 px gives the answer of the rig of one size
    with the same noise:
    - f within 4e-5;
    - the principal point within 0.09 px;
    - the distances between the cameras within 3e-5;
    - the rotations between them within 5e-5 rad.

    This holds whether that camera starts the fit (it is in the best pair) or joins later.
  - **With two cameras** the answers agree within the calibrator's own numerical noise (D3; section 3).
- **B12: cameras numbered with a gap work.** The ids 0, 2, 3 give the answer of 0, 1, 2 to the bit. The camFiles are
  byte-identical, and the error table and the results page show the right ids.
- **Cameras of one size are unchanged to the bit:** three cameras, two cameras, and Precalibrate's check (X1).
- **The "fast" mode in air:**
  - **Accuracy:** a median centre error of 0.07 px.
  - **Repeatability:** two runs differ by at most 0.04 px.
  - **Speed:** 0.13 s per image.
  - **Against "reliable":** 0.16 px and 4.2 s, so 30 times slower and half as accurate.
  - **Where it fails:** its big jumps (up to 27 px) happen only on balls seen through a tube, where the guide already
    says to use "ellipse".
- **Found (not changed):**
  - **The Pinhole model's principal point is numerically weak.** A 1e-9 px change of the data moves it by 0.3 px with
    three cameras. With two cameras, a 1e-6 px change moves it by 1.9 px (D3). This is the calibration's own
    property, not the fix's.
  - **A trap for scripts:** `run_detection_task` returns no points, silently, when the C++ module cannot be imported
    (D2's first try). The GUI is not affected.

## 4. The fast mode (your question)

**D2:** 60 synthetic images of a wand in air (1280 × 800 px; balls of 22–30 and 38–50 px radius, a 6 px rod and a
handle; edge blur 1.5 px, noise 2 grey levels, a backlight gradient), with known centres. The modes are run as the GUI
runs them.

| mode | both balls found | centre error, median / 95 % / largest | radius error (median) | time per image | two runs differ by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fast | 60 of 60 | 0.07 / 0.21 / 0.29 px | 0.60 px | 0.13 s | at most 0.04 px |
| reliable | 60 of 60 | 0.16 / 0.34 / 0.45 px | 0.53 px | 4.2 s | 0 |
| ellipse | 60 of 60 | 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.06 px | 0.56 px | 0.16 s | 0 |

**On W1's T2 frames (balls through the tube, no truth):**
- **fast:** two runs differed by up to 27 px; one run kept another blob.
- **reliable:** up to 5 points per image.
- **ellipse:** at most 2.

(The times were measured while pytest ran beside them, so they are somewhat high; the ratios hold. The first try
ran without the C++ module on the path, where "reliable" silently found nothing: `d2_fast_mode_first_try_out.txt`.)

**My answer: keep "fast".** It is the right mode for balls that image as circles:
- it is twice as accurate as "reliable" and 30 times faster;
- its random draws change its centres by at most 0.04 px.

Its large jumps come from using it on ellipses, through a tube, and for those the guide and the tube model's results
page already point to "ellipse". Removing it would leave "reliable" as the circle detector: slower and less
accurate.

**Your options:**
1. **Keep it as it is.** The guide's "Known issues" now gives these numbers.
2. **Keep it and make it repeatable (my recommendation):**
   - **The change:** a fixed-seed random generator inside `refine_circle_with_edges`, in place of numpy's global one.
   - **The effect:** the same image then gives the same points every time, in Test Detect and in Process All Frames
     alike.
   - **The cost:** the points change once, by about 0.04 px on images in air.
3. **Remove it:** not recommended, for the numbers above.

**Separately:** "ellipse" was the most accurate even in air (0.02 px), at about the speed of "fast". Before it could
become the default, it should be checked on real images of a wand in air: its rod settings must suit the rod.

## 7. B13: the fast mode, seeded (your choice, option 2)

**The change:** `refine_circle_with_edges`, the fast mode's RANSAC circle fit, draws its samples from its own
generator, `np.random.default_rng(FAST_RANSAC_SEED)`, in place of numpy's global random state (`np.random.choice`).
- **The seed:** `FAST_RANSAC_SEED = 0`, a new module constant.
- **What it gives:** every circle's draws are the same, so an image's points depend only on the image and the
  settings. They no longer depend on the run, the process, or which of Test Detect and Process All Frames ran it.
- **What it leaves alone:** numpy's global state is no longer touched.
- **The only other users:** the cylinder campaign's T13 scripts, which call `detect_circles_robust`. Their archived
  fast-mode numbers came from unseeded draws, so they were never exactly reproducible; a rerun now gives fixed
  numbers.

| test | what | result |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | D2's 60 images and W1's 12 T2 frames: twice in one process with numpy's global seed set to 1, then 2; the global state before and after; fresh worker processes through Test Detect's path (6 + 6 images) and Process All Frames' (the 60 D2 images as two cameras) | **7 of 7.** The same points to the bit everywhere; the global state unchanged |
| S2 | D2's images: the seeded points against four unseeded runs of git HEAD's detector | **2 of 2.** The seeded points differ from the unseeded runs by 0.04–0.06 px; two unseeded runs differed by 0.04–0.05 px. Accuracy unchanged: 0.072 / 0.214 / 0.294 px (median, 95 %, largest); every ball found |
| S3 | W1 rerun; D2 rerun; pytest (`w11_s3_*`) | **W1 7 of 7:** fast's Test Detect check is now identical to the bit (6 of 6 frames; before, only within the random spread). **D2:** a second fast run changes nothing (0.000 px; also on the T2 frames, where it had jumped by 27 px); the accuracy table is unchanged. **pytest:** 254 passed, the same four old failures and errors |
