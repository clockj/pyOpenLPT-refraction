## 3. A window's thickness cannot be seen by the wand

**What failed.**
- **P2's case A** expected a 2.7 mm thickness error (12.7 mm entered for a 10 mm window) to fit worse. It fits exactly
  as well: RMS 0.11654 px both, f error 0.299 % both.
- **P2b** then measured what does change:
  - **The cameras, f, the residuals and the wand ends:** the same to the last digit. The ends in the output frame differ
    by 0.0 µm.
  - **The window's surfaces:** the camera-side surface moved 0.43 mm and the water-side surface 3.1 mm (the extra
    thickness).
  - **With the right thickness,** the water-side surface lies 1.5–2.2 mm from the truth. That is the cameras' distance
    precision here (f within 0.3 %, about 1.7 mm at 580 mm).
  - **With the wrong one** it lies 4.7–5.3 mm from the truth.
- **P2b's two criteria** expected the camera side within 0.5 mm, and the window to move toward the cameras. Both were
  wrong, and are reported as failed.

**What it means.**
- A flat window's thickness trades off exactly against where it sits: the rays in the water stay the same.
- **Positions in the water** (tracking) do not depend on the entered thickness.
- **Anything measured from the window's surfaces does:** the channel constraint (depths below the water-side surface),
  aims, and the reported distances.
- **So enter each window's measured thickness;** the wand cannot correct it. The Windows table now allows a different
  value per window.
