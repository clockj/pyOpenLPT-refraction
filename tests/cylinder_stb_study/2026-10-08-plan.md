## 1. How T1 differs from the synthetic case

| | synthetic (r5_lines, R3) | T1 (final run) |
|---|---|---|
| where the particles are | within 30 mm of the axis only | throughout the 195 mm tube |
| images per camera per frame | 1,696 / 2,154 | 1,491 / 2,088 objects at 4σ; 1,000 / 860 detected |
| images with a partner in the other camera | 55 % / 43 % (936 particles in both) | about 25 % (≈ 250 true pairs per frame) |
| true matches within 1 px of the epipolar curve | all (the tracker has the true cameras) | 65 %; 90 % within 2 px (T0's error pattern) |
| shape model | exact (the images are rendered from it) | fitted from T1; 68 % within 0.4–0.9 px, a trend with depth, wide tails |
| noise | white, 7 counts | 8.1 / 5.9 counts, plus a texture of faint, blurred particles (long templates 3.5–8.6× noisier than white noise) |
| flux | constant (× 0.6–1.4 per particle) | grows 6–9× with the image's size |
| result | recall 0.80, ghosts 6.4 % | ghosts about 44–51 % (time-shifted control) |

Candidate reasons, in the order I expect them to matter:
1. **Pairing.** Particles fill the whole tube, and each camera sees its own slab through it. Only one detection in
   four has a partner, against one in two in the synthetic case. This also makes the faint texture.
2. **Calibration.** T0's error pattern spreads the true matches over ±2 px. That forces a 1.5 px tolerance, which
   admits more random pairs.
3. **Shape model.** On T1 the depth cue keeps half the true pairs at 3 % of the random ones. With an exact model
   it should keep nearly all of them.
4. **Detection at T1's density.** Recall fell to 80 % / 51 % in the T1-like synthetic set of the lines report.

Part G tests these one at a time.

## 3. Part S: synthetic tracking with 2, 3 and 4 cameras, at three noise levels

| rig | cameras |
|---|---|

| 3 | + cam3, between them at 30° azimuth, 25° above the horizontal, looking down at the volume |
| 4 | + cam4, at 30° azimuth, 25° below |

The elevated cameras see the tube's axis at an angle, so their epipolar curves cross the horizontal ones. That
breaks the 2-camera ambiguity, where every curve runs along the same direction.

**Particles and flow:**

  6; T1's is about 7. The report gives the measured values.

**Runs:**

- **Today's code** at σ = 8 on each rig (3 runs), to see whether more cameras rescue it.

**Scoring:** `eval_truth.py`, extended to n cameras. Recall counts particles seen by all cameras and, separately,
by at least n − 1 and at least 2. Also ghosts, position error, track recall and purity, and time per frame.

**Judged:** reported, with the trends against cameras and noise. No pass/fail target; you set targets if you want

## 4. Part G: why T1 tracks worse than the synthetic case

**G1. The same statistics on both.** On the synthetic case, with the truth, the statistics T1 can only estimate:
- the share of detections with a partner;
- the spread of the true matches across the epipolar curves;
- the depth cue's true and random pairs kept;
- the detection recall.

Side by side with T1's, this gives a gap table.

**G2. The synthetic case made realistic one factor at a time.** Each is its own rendered case with 2 cameras at
σ = 8, tracked with this work's settings, with the tracker unchanged:

| case | added to the clean synthetic case |
|---|---|
| G-cal | a smooth image-plane error field per camera, 0.7 px RMS like T0's pattern, which the tracker does not know |
| G-tube | particles throughout the tube, at a density that gives T1's images per camera |
| G-shape | per-particle scatter of the blur lengths (±25 %); flux growing with the image's size as on T1; T1's fitted A, z_f and σ0 |
| G-all | all three |

G-tube needs no separate texture: particles far from focus give the texture by themselves.

Each case is scored as in Part S, plus G1's statistics.

**G3. The ghost estimate checked.** The time-shifted control is run on G-all, where the truth is known. Does the
control's estimate, the method behind T1's 44–51 %, match the true ghost share?

**Judged:** which factor costs the most recall and adds the most ghosts. G-all should land near T1's numbers. If it
does not, something else is missing, and the report says what.

**Your question: yes, the tracker filters by the view volume** (`view_volume_mm`; the code, build p4c):
- stereo matching draws each epipolar line or curve only over the part of the line of sight inside the volume
  (`StereoMatch::makeLine2DFromLOS3D`, `makeCurve2DFromLOS3D`), and rejects a match whose triangulated point lies
  outside it (`StereoMatch.cpp`, the `limit.check` calls);
- STB ends a track when its predicted position leaves the volume or fewer than two cameras see it (`STB.cpp`, the
  prediction step).

The filter acts on 3-D points only. The images of particles outside the volume stay in the images: they are
detected, their lines of sight cross the volume, and they can pair by chance with images in the other camera into
ghosts inside it. They also overlap images of particles inside the volume, and they stay in the residual images that
the shake step works on. Part O measures what that costs.
